## What the official schemes support

- **E051:** ethical guidelines and known standards, reputation/recognition or entry requirements, professional help or legal advice, and training to update skills. Each accepted point earns one mark, up to three. The teaching links a benefit to its practical value. The scheme does not establish that all membership grades or bodies promise identical services.
- **E052:** expectations of conduct, consequences, good practice or situation-specific rules, safe professional working, and public trust or reputation. Two accepted purpose points earn the available marks. A conduct code is not program source code.
- **E053:** fair treatment, constructive criticism, acknowledgement of contributions, help with development, protection of health/safety or personal information, and honest claims about software. There is a **two-mark cap for colleagues and a separate two-mark cap for the public**. The lesson develops actions and their effects; teacher-created alternative scenarios are not extra official marking points.
- **E7D03B:** privacy, avoiding cyberbullying and plagiarism, following network rules, lawful network use and avoiding unauthorised access. The question asks for three **other** considerations, because the opening context already supplies malware.
- **E054B:** identification of the author or ownership, legal consequences for unauthorised copying, and the scheme’s competition-related point. The main explanation develops ownership and legal protection. Encouraging investment can be discussed as teacher explanation, but is not claimed as another point listed by this specific MS.
- **E054A:** one mark for the intended category and up to two for accepted reasons, including source access/modification and the developer’s ability to earn income. The original scheme names OSI/FSF. These are organisations associated with the categories, not two individual licence documents.
- **E7D01:** shareware may allow trying before purchase, no initial cost, promotion by users and feedback. Commercial distribution may earn fees and usually provide support or well-tested software. Each part has a two-mark cap. There is no required one-user/one-developer split, and support/testing benefits are typical rather than universal guarantees.
- **E055:** image recognition/OCR converts image patterns to characters, language processing interprets/combines/translates text, and text-to-speech produces audio. The process has a four-mark cap; two social benefits have a separate two-mark cap. Benefits include help for people with visual impairments, language barriers or reading/learning difficulties. Its later licensing subquestion is not selected.
- **E7D02:** one developed ethical impact may concern identification of struggling students and timely support, personalisation, privacy or data sharing, pressure or anxiety. The question does not demand one positive and one negative impact.
- **E7D03C:** extra learning support, own-paced learning/confidence, less independent reasoning or collaboration, unequal access, inaccurate information, isolation/anxiety and other listed impacts. The MS awards accepted bullets up to four marks in total; it does not impose a fixed two-mark cap per impact or a compulsory positive/negative quota.

## Concept sources and terminology

The diagrams are original editable HTML/SVG, with explanatory text checked against these primary sources:

- [BCS Code of Conduct](https://www.bcs.org/membership-and-registrations/become-a-member/bcs-code-of-conduct/): professional duties, public interest, competence and integrity. The diagram introduces the organisation; the exam membership table is separately based on E051.
- [IEEE Code of Ethics, hosted by the IEEE Circuits and Systems Society](https://ieee-cas.org/about/ieee-code-ethics): professional integrity, public welfare, honest claims, competence, fair treatment and support for ethical conduct. The central IEEE page was blocked to the web retrieval tool; this official society reproduction was read instead. IEEE’s full name is Institute of Electrical and **Electronics** Engineers.
- [FSF’s four-freedoms handout](https://static.fsf.org/nosvn/posters/handout-four-freedoms.pdf) and [FSF’s explanation of free software](https://www.fsf.org/about/what-is-free-software): running, studying/changing, sharing original copies, and sharing modified versions. Source access supports study and modification. Freedom is distinct from a zero purchase price.
- [Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd): source access plus modification and redistribution permissions, with applicable licence conditions. Merely publishing readable source is insufficient. The source explicitly permits commercial use and redistribution; commercial and proprietary are different properties.
- [WIPO copyright FAQ](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/faq-copyright): copyright protects expression rather than ideas, including program expression; protection generally arises without mandatory registration. E054’s wording must not become a claim that every programmer has to register a copyright.
- Google’s primary introductory material on [machine learning](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/intro-to-ml/what-is-ml), [supervised learning](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/intro-to-ml/supervised), and its [AI glossary](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/glossary#artificial_intelligence): concept support for models, learning from examples, neural networks and learning categories. The AI material separates architecture from learning feedback, and training from later inference. Its invented labelled records, reward example and numerical comparisons are teacher-written, not empirical data or official marking schemes.

The software comparison deliberately distinguishes permissions, trial distribution and business activity. Its columns are overlapping descriptions, not four mutually exclusive legal categories. Actual support, term, deployment and redistribution rights must be read from the offered licence and service agreement.

## Search scope and assessment gaps

On 2026-11-16, the local archive was searched again across **18 Paper 1 question papers**: all three variants in May/June and Oct/Nov for 2023, 2024 and 2025. Ten AI-related question locations were found; they addressed recognition, speech, applications, social impacts or ethical impacts. No direct question on AI’s **economic or environmental impacts**, or on **machine/supervised/unsupervised/reinforcement learning**, was found in this bounded sample. This does not claim that Cambridge has never assessed those topics elsewhere.

Economic and environmental impacts remain AS syllabus requirements and are taught with explicitly labelled teacher-written explanation and transfer checks. Learning terminology and relations are concept-level supporting material: detailed ML/DL/RL, supervised/unsupervised learning, neural networks, back propagation and regression occur in A Level Section 18.1. The S7 route does not introduce the A Level mathematical or algorithmic depth as an AS requirement.

The selected real questions also do not independently assess every FSF freedom, the names of both professional bodies, or a complete ethical-decision framework. Dedicated teacher-written checks cover those aspects. Source coverage does not determine syllabus importance.
