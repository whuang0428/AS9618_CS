Knowledge point 1

Wired transmission media

Wired media carry signals through a physical cable. They are usually suitable for fixed devices and controlled environments.

Copper cable

Copper cable transmits data using electrical signals. It is common in local networks because it is relatively cheap and easy to install.

  • Good for: short local links, desktop PCs, classroom networks.
  • Limits: affected by electromagnetic interference; lower bandwidth and distance than fibre in many contexts.
  • Exam wording: say "electrical signals", not "data flows like water". The cable is not a tiny plumbing system.

Fibre-optic cable

Fibre-optic cable transmits data as pulses of light through glass or plastic fibres.

  • Good for: high-bandwidth links, long distances, backbone connections.
  • Strengths: less affected by electromagnetic interference and can support high data rates.
  • Limits: installation can be more expensive and the cable can be more delicate to handle.
Chinese support: copper cable = 铜缆；fibre-optic cable = 光纤；electrical signal = 电信号；light pulse = 光脉冲。

Knowledge point 2

Wireless transmission media

Wireless media transmit through the air or space. They support mobility, but the signal can be affected by distance, obstacles and interference.

Radio waves

Radio waves are used by technologies such as WiFi to transmit data without a physical cable.

  • Good for: mobile devices, classrooms, homes, short-range wireless networking.
  • Limits: shared medium, interference from other devices, weaker security if poorly configured.

Microwaves

Microwave links can transmit data between fixed points, often using directional antennas.

  • Good for: point-to-point links where a line of sight is available.
  • Limits: obstacles, weather and alignment can affect the link.

Satellites

Satellite communication uses signals to and from satellites to cover large geographical areas.

  • Good for: remote locations, ships, aircraft or areas without cable infrastructure.
  • Limits: higher latency due to distance, cost, weather effects and limited capacity compared with some wired links.
Chinese support: radio wave = 无线电波；microwave = 微波；satellite = 卫星；line of sight = 视距。

Mistake debugging

Fix the weak transmission-media answer

"Wireless is always faster because it is newer."

Do not judge by age. Wireless performance depends on signal strength, interference, congestion and distance; fibre may provide higher stable bandwidth.

"Fibre-optic cable sends electrical signals very quickly."

Fibre-optic cable transmits data as pulses of light, not electrical signals. Copper cable uses electrical signals.

Homework

Clear tasks

  1. Create a comparison table for copper, fibre optic, radio waves, microwaves and satellites. Include one advantage and one limitation for each.
  2. Write a 4-mark answer: explain why fibre optic may be chosen for a high-bandwidth link between buildings.
  3. Write a 4-mark answer: explain why WiFi may be chosen for tablets in a classroom, and give one limitation.
  4. Choose a medium for a remote weather station and justify it using coverage, latency or cost.

Fibre answer: mention high bandwidth, long distance and low electromagnetic interference.

WiFi answer: mention mobility/no cable, then a limitation such as interference, security or shared bandwidth.
