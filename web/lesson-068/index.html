Lesson 068 | 45 minutes | Paper 1 Section 6 | Security, privacy and data integrity

Network security is not just one locked door.

Firewalls filter traffic using rules. Proxies sit between client and destination to forward, filter, cache or log requests. Network monitoring observes traffic and events so unusual behaviour can be detected and investigated.

Explainhow firewalls, proxies and monitoring work
Applyrules and logs to realistic network scenarios
Evaluatebenefits and limits without claiming any control is magic
Firewall -> allow/block traffic Proxy -> intermediary request handler Monitoring -> observe and alert Logs -> evidence for investigation

A firewall is a filter, not a cybersecurity force field.

Why this matters

Exam answers should say what traffic is inspected or logged and what decision is made.

Common trap

Monitoring detects suspicious activity; it does not automatically prevent every attack unless linked to a response.

Warm-up hook

A school blocks outgoing traffic to port 25 except from the mail server. What control is doing the work?

The network is saying, "Not everyone gets to run their own postal service from a backpack."

Choose one. The clue is filtering by traffic rule.

Knowledge point 1

Network controls sit between traffic and risk

TrafficData packets or requests moving across a network.
RuleA condition that allows, blocks or logs traffic based on properties.
LogA record of events, traffic, blocked attempts or user activity.
AlertA warning generated when traffic or behaviour matches a suspicious pattern.
Chinese support: firewall = 防火墙；proxy = 代理服务器；network monitoring = 网络监控；log = 日志。

Knowledge point 2

Firewalls filter traffic using rules

PurposeControl traffic entering or leaving a network/device.
ChecksCan inspect source/destination IP address, port number, protocol or connection state.
DecisionAllow, deny, reject or log traffic depending on rule match.
PlacementCan protect a network boundary or run on an individual host.

Knowledge point 4

A proxy acts as an intermediary

Forward requestsClient sends request to proxy; proxy requests the resource from the destination.
FilteringCan block sites, content types, categories or requests that break policy.
CachingCan store copies of frequently requested resources to reduce bandwidth and latency.
LoggingCan record user requests for audit, investigation or policy enforcement.

Knowledge point 5

Network monitoring watches for patterns and evidence

Traffic volumeUnusual spikes may suggest DoS, malware activity or misconfiguration.
Connection attemptsRepeated failed access attempts may suggest probing or brute force activity.
AlertsMonitoring systems can notify staff when thresholds or signatures are triggered.
InvestigationLogs help reconstruct what happened, when, and from where.

Knowledge point 6

Controls reduce risk but have limits

Firewall limitAllowed traffic can still carry attacks, and rules may be misconfigured.
Proxy limitEncrypted traffic and unmanaged devices can reduce visibility if not configured properly.
Monitoring limitAlerts need review and response; too many false positives can be ignored.
LayeringUse with authentication, permissions, patching, encryption and user training.

Summary

Each control has a different job

FirewallFilters traffic by rules such as port, protocol, address or state.
ProxyActs as intermediary for requests; can filter, cache and log.
MonitoringObserves traffic/events and produces alerts or evidence.
