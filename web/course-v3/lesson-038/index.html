Build a reading assistant people can trust

Ethics and ownership · Concept 01

Why does technical knowledge create responsibility?

Observe

Look, then discuss

The assistant speaks confidently. Who knows whether it read the page correctly?

Compare the printed instruction with the spoken output in the diagram. A student may rely on the spoken words because they cannot easily read the page. The developer has recorded a failure, but the student may not know about it. Decide who needs that evidence before the tool is used.

Recall

Use what you know about data and access

A digital image represents pixels; text represents characters; an audio output represents sound. You do not yet need to know how the assistant converts between them. Recall also that an account can allow a person to access data without deciding whether every possible use of that access is appropriate.

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 4

Begin with people depending on an output

A computing professional is someone whose work uses specialist computing knowledge, such as a developer, systems analyst or consultant. A student may depend on the reading assistant without understanding its source code. The developer can investigate faults and recognise limitations that the student cannot independently discover.

Explanation · 2 / 4

Explain ethics in this situation

Ethics concerns principles about right and wrong conduct. Professional ethics guides decisions about how specialist knowledge and control should be used. Here, the developer must consider whether the student can rely on the output, not merely whether the program starts and produces sound.

Explanation · 3 / 4

Connect knowledge to a duty

Suppose the developer knows that blurred pages can produce incorrect words. Concealing this limitation leaves the school with an incomplete basis for deciding how to use the tool. Explaining the limitation lets the teacher check uncertain readings or use a different resource. The professional has a responsibility because others depend on knowledge that the professional controls.

Explanation · 4 / 4

State the purposes of professional ethics

Ethics helps protect the public interest, encourages honest and competent work and makes professionals accountable for their decisions. Accountability means being answerable for what was done and why. These purposes support justified trust: people can rely on evidence and responsible conduct instead of an impressive claim alone.

Work together

A confident reading of an uncertain page

During a trial, the assistant sometimes changes a printed instruction from “Do not enter” to “Do enter”. The developer has recorded the failure. The school is about to use the assistant for safety signs.

Work together · 1 / 2

Identify the dependence

A student who relies on speech may act on the spoken instruction without checking the original sign. The developer knows the demonstrated limitation, while the student and school may not.

Work together · 2 / 2

Choose a responsible action and explain it

Report the failed readings and advise against relying on this version for safety instructions. Arrange a checked alternative while the fault is investigated. This gives the school relevant evidence and protects people who might otherwise follow an incorrect instruction.

The need for ethics follows from how professional decisions affect people who depend on specialist work.

Check

Explain it yourself · Teacher-written

Change the case: the same error appears in a poem rather than a safety sign. Does the professional responsibility disappear?

Check your reasoning

No. Honest reporting and appropriate use remain necessary. The likely harm and urgency change, so the response can be proportionate: a checked reading may be sufficient for the poem, while safety information needs a more restrictive response. Do not pretend that both contexts have identical consequences.

Connect

Keep this idea

Professional ethics guides the use of specialist knowledge to protect people, support honest work and establish accountability.

We now need to turn these broad purposes into actions a professional can take.

Check a common misunderstanding

A successful demonstration or technically permitted action does not establish that every use is responsible.

Learning goals
  • Explain the need for and purpose of ethics as a computing professional.

Ethics and ownership · Concept 02

Turn professional duties into practical actions

Observe

Look, then discuss

What should a developer do when a manager asks for a misleading test report?

The reading assistant failed three tests, but the manager wants the report to say “all tests passed” so that delivery is on time. Name the fact that would be hidden and the person who needs it. Then consider a second issue: the report contains identifiable student reading records.

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 4

Work within your competence

Competence means having the knowledge and skills required for the work. A developer who has not tested a language should not claim expertise or accuracy for that language. They should state the limitation and seek a suitably qualified review. Recognising a limit is part of competent practice.

Explanation · 2 / 4

Report evidence honestly

Honesty means reporting results, limitations and uncertainty accurately. An observed failure should be described as a failure, while an unconfirmed report should be described as a concern requiring investigation. Meeting a deadline does not justify changing a failed result into a passed one.

Explanation · 3 / 4

Protect confidentiality and disclose conflicts

Confidentiality means avoiding inappropriate disclosure of information entrusted to the professional. The school needs the failure evidence, but an unrestricted public post containing student records can create a separate harm. Integrity also requires disclosing a personal interest, such as a commission from the recommended supplier, so that advice can be evaluated fairly.

Explanation · 4 / 4

Separate law, instructions and ethical judgement

Law sets enforceable requirements. An employer gives work instructions, and a professional code sets expectations of conduct. These may support the same action, but obeying a manager or finding no explicit prohibition does not settle whether an action is responsible. Equally, identifying an ethical concern does not by itself prove that a law has been broken.

Work together

Report the fault without exposing student records

A developer must respond to the request for an “all passed” report. The cause of the reading failure has not yet been confirmed.

Work together · 1 / 3

Record what is known

Keep the actual test conditions and results. State that failures occurred and that their cause remains under investigation. Do not invent a diagnosis or remove the failed cases from the summary.

Work together · 2 / 3

Choose the appropriate audience and material

Notify the responsible school and development leads using a report with only the information they need. Protect identifiable student records and use a suitable authorised reporting route.

Work together · 3 / 3

Follow the concern through

Recommend correction and retesting before relying on the affected function. If further expertise is needed, request it. Record the new evidence rather than promising that no future error is possible.

Competence, honesty, confidentiality and integrity need specific actions, recipients and reasons.

Check

Explain it yourself · Teacher-written

A consultant receives an undisclosed commission for recommending the assistant. The assistant works correctly. Is there still an ethical concern?

Check your reasoning

Yes. The financial interest can affect the independence of the recommendation even when the software functions. Disclose the commission and arrange an impartial comparison with the school’s requirements. A conflict of interest does not require a defective product.

Connect

Keep this idea

Explain the duty, the action it requires and the person it protects.

Professional bodies provide shared standards and support for applying these duties.

Check a common misunderstanding

A duty to report a concern does not automatically justify publishing all confidential information associated with it.

Learning goals
  • Apply professional duties and distinguish ethical judgement from legal compliance.

Ethics and ownership · Concept 03

Use professional standards and support

Observe

Look, then discuss

Where can a new developer find recognised standards when an employer asks them to hide a fault?

Compare three kinds of help: a written conduct standard, a training event and advice from an experienced professional. Identify which helps establish expected behaviour, which develops competence and which helps someone respond to a difficult situation.

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 4

Identify the two professional bodies

BCS is the British Computer Society. IEEE is the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers. They are professional organisations that support standards and professional practice. They are different from a software supplier, an employee’s manager or the team testing a particular product.

Explanation · 2 / 4

Explain what a code of conduct does

A professional code states the behaviour and standards expected of members. It gives professionals and the public a reference against which conduct can be judged. Associated conduct procedures provide accountability and consequences for breaches. Program source code, in contrast, contains instructions for a computer.

Explanation · 3 / 4

Explain why membership helps a practitioner

Continuing professional development helps a member keep knowledge and skills current. Technical information and professional contacts help a developer recognise limits and obtain advice. Ethical guidance helps them explain why hiding a known fault conflicts with professional responsibilities. State how a benefit helps the person’s work instead of listing a benefit without a reason.

Explanation · 4 / 4

Keep support separate from proof

Membership can support professional recognition and public confidence, but it does not certify that every member’s decision is ethical. The member still chooses how to act. The assistant still needs appropriate design, testing and review; a membership logo is not evidence that it reads every page correctly.

Work together

Use membership when the work becomes unfamiliar

A new programmer is asked to approve a speech feature in a language they do not understand and to sign an inaccurate test report.

Work together · 1 / 2

Connect learning and advice to competence

Training and technical contacts can help the programmer understand the feature and find a suitably qualified reviewer. The programmer should still disclose their present lack of expertise.

Work together · 2 / 2

Connect the code to the conduct problem

The programmer can refer to the expected honesty and public-interest standards when explaining why the actual test results must be reported. Guidance supports that response; it does not make the inaccurate report acceptable or transfer responsibility to the body.

A code states expected conduct. Membership provides ways to maintain competence and obtain professional support.

Check

Explain it yourself · Teacher-written

Give one reason to join a professional body and one purpose of its code. Explain why neither guarantees that the reading assistant is safe.

Check your reasoning

Membership can provide training that keeps the programmer’s skills current. The code states expected professional behaviour and supports accountability for breaches. The programmer may still make poor decisions, and the product’s behaviour must be established through relevant evidence and testing.

Original question · Cambridge 9618/13

1. Explain professional membership

Cambridge 9618/13 · October/November 2024 · 5(b) · 3 marks

Selected parts: 5(b) [3].

Hint — reading the question

Give reasons for a programmer to join a professional ethical body. Connect the support or standards to the programmer’s work.

Reasoning and model answer — teacher-written

The body supplies ethical guidelines so the programmer and clients know the standards expected of the work. It can provide training that keeps the programmer’s skills up to date. It can also offer professional support, including advice when ethical problems arise.

Official mark scheme
Why this earns marks — teacher explanation

The maximum is three, with one mark for each accepted point. The scheme also accepts reputation, entry requirements, recognition and legal advice; naming several bodies earns no extra credit.

Common mistakes — teacher analysis

“It is good for programmers” does not identify a benefit. A professional body is not the same as the programmer’s employer.

Original question · Cambridge 9618/12

2. State the purpose of a code of conduct

Cambridge 9618/12 · October/November 2025 · 2(b) · 2 marks

Selected parts: 2(b) [2].

Hint — reading the question

Describe what the code establishes. You do not need to invent a disciplinary case or list an organisation’s rules.

Reasoning and model answer — teacher-written

A code of conduct sets out the behaviour and professional standards expected of members. It also explains the consequences of failing to follow those standards.

Official mark scheme
Why this earns marks — teacher explanation

Two accepted purpose statements earn the two available marks. The scheme also accepts promoting safe professional work, good practice and public trust.

Common mistakes — teacher analysis

A code of conduct guides professional behaviour; program source code instructs a computer.

Connect

Keep this idea

Know BCS and IEEE, distinguish a code’s purpose from membership benefits, and explain each benefit in context.

With the duties established, we can compare the effects of different actions on specific people.

Check a common misunderstanding

BCS and IEEE are professional bodies. They are not software licences, employers or guarantees of product quality.

Learning goals
  • Identify BCS and IEEE as professional bodies.
  • Explain why joining a professional ethical body supports professional practice.

Question 1 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

Explain two reasons why professional ethics is needed when designing a system that stores private patient records.

S7.01.A01
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Patients depend on specialists to protect information they cannot directly control, so professional duties help prevent misuse of that access.
  2. Rules may not resolve every proposed use of the records, so ethical principles help the designer judge effects on patients.

Common error: Develop each reason using the records context.

Question 2 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

An employee is asked to sign off a system outside their expertise. Explain why obeying the instruction alone is insufficient and suggest a responsible action.

S7.01.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Approval without the necessary competence could give others unjustified confidence in the system.
  2. The employee should disclose the limit of their expertise and seek a suitably qualified review.

Common error: Link the action to the competence problem.

Question 3 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

Explain two ways joining a professional body could help a programmer keep their skills current and respond to pressure to hide a fault.

S7.02.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Continuing professional development can help the programmer learn current techniques and recognise limits in their knowledge.
  2. A professional code gives the programmer recognised standards with which to explain why the fault should be reported.

Common error: Connect each membership benefit to one of the two stated needs.

Question 4 · Command word: State2 teacher marks

State two purposes of ethics in the computing profession.

S7.01.A01
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Protect the public interest when computing decisions affect people.
  2. Promote trustworthy, accountable professional conduct.

Common error: Two restatements of 'doing the right thing' are not two distinct purposes.

Question 5 · Command word: Identify2 teacher marks

Identify the professional bodies abbreviated BCS and IEEE.

S7.02.A01
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. BCS: British Computer Society.
  2. IEEE: Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers.
