Professional ethics and professional bodies

Explain why computing professionals need ethical principles, apply their duties, and recognise how professional bodies support responsible practice.

What should a computing professional do when a requested action could harm users?

Before you start

An employee can read a customer record using a valid account. Does access permission alone justify every use of it?

Check your answer

No. Access permission does not establish that a use is appropriate; consider the purpose, affected people and professional responsibilities.

01

Why computing professionals need ethics

S7.01.A01

Visual overview

Core explanation

Ethics concerns principles about right and wrong conduct. A computing professional makes decisions that can affect other people's safety, privacy, opportunities and resources. Users may lack the technical knowledge or access needed to detect an unsafe system or challenge its design, so technical power brings responsibilities.

Professional ethics helps guide decisions when interests conflict or a rule does not settle what should be done. Its purposes include protecting the public interest, promoting honest and competent work, maintaining trust and making professionals accountable for foreseeable effects of their decisions.

Common misconception

A technically possible action is not automatically an ethical action.

Check your understanding

Why is ethics relevant even when a customer cannot detect an unsafe design?

Check your answer

The professional's responsibility to avoid foreseeable harm does not depend on whether the customer can discover the problem.

Professional duties and legal compliance

S7.01.A02

Core explanation

A professional should work within their competence, report limitations honestly, protect confidential information and consider effects on the public. Conflicting interests should be disclosed so that advice can be evaluated fairly. For example, a paid relationship with a supplier can influence a recommendation even when that recommendation is technically possible.

Law sets enforceable requirements; ethical judgement also considers what is responsible in the particular circumstances. An employer's instruction or the absence of an explicit legal prohibition does not remove a professional's duty to consider harm. A defensible response records the concern, seeks appropriate guidance and proposes an action that addresses it.

Worked example · An untested warning system

  1. Request

    A developer is asked to describe a building's new fire-warning interface as fully tested, although the warning display has failed one test.

  2. Duty

    Occupants depend on that warning. An honest report must include the failed test and its safety implications.

  3. Response

    Record the fault, notify the responsible safety lead and recommend correcting and retesting the display before relying on it.

  4. Reason

    A manager's wish to meet a deadline does not justify concealing evidence on which a safety decision depends.

Common misconception

Ethical reasoning requires an explanation of the duty and its consequences, not simply the label 'legal' or 'illegal'.

Check your understanding

A manager requests an inaccurate test report. Who remains responsible for writing it?

Check your answer

The professional remains accountable for their own report and should raise the concern rather than treat the instruction as sufficient justification.

BCS, IEEE and the value of membership

Core explanation

BCS is the British Computer Society. IEEE is the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers. These professional bodies provide codes of conduct or ethics that express standards expected of members. A shared code gives a practitioner a reference when explaining a professional concern to an employer or client.

Joining can provide continuing professional development, technical information and contact with experienced peers. These help members maintain competence and obtain guidance when a problem exceeds their experience. Membership also brings expectations of conduct and accountability through the body's professional processes, supporting public confidence in the profession.

A code supports judgement but cannot guarantee that every member acts ethically. Membership does not replace the law, remove personal responsibility or automatically certify every product a member develops.

Common misconception

Naming a professional body does not explain why joining it matters.

Check your understanding

Does a developer's IEEE membership prove that their application is safe?

Check your answer

No. Membership supports professional standards; the application's safety still needs appropriate design, testing and review.

Cambridge command words

Practice questions

Question 1 · Command word: Explain2 marks

Explain two reasons why professional ethics is needed when designing a system that stores private patient records.

S7.01.A01
Answer and marking points
  1. Patients depend on specialists to protect information they cannot directly control, so professional duties help prevent misuse of that access.
  2. Rules may not resolve every proposed use of the records, so ethical principles help the designer judge effects on patients.

Common error: Develop each reason using the records context.

Question 2 · Command word: State2 marks

State two purposes of ethics in the computing profession.

S7.01.A01
Answer and marking points
  1. Protect the public interest when computing decisions affect people.
  2. Promote trustworthy, accountable professional conduct.

Common error: Two restatements of 'doing the right thing' are not two distinct purposes.

Question 3 · Command word: Explain2 marks

An employee is asked to sign off a system outside their expertise. Explain why obeying the instruction alone is insufficient and suggest a responsible action.

S7.01.A02
Answer and marking points
  1. Approval without the necessary competence could give others unjustified confidence in the system.
  2. The employee should disclose the limit of their expertise and seek a suitably qualified review.

Common error: Link the action to the competence problem.

Question 4 · Command word: Identify2 marks

Identify the professional bodies abbreviated BCS and IEEE.

S7.02.A01
Answer and marking points
  1. BCS: British Computer Society.
  2. IEEE: Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers.

Common error: Give the organisation names rather than examples of software companies.

Question 5 · Command word: Explain2 marks

Explain two ways joining a professional body could help a programmer keep their skills current and respond to pressure to hide a fault.

S7.02.A02
Answer and marking points
  1. Continuing professional development can help the programmer learn current techniques and recognise limits in their knowledge.
  2. A professional code gives the programmer recognised standards with which to explain why the fault should be reported.

Common error: Connect each membership benefit to one of the two stated needs.

Question 1 · Command word: Explain · 3 marks

A contractor discovers that a bridge-monitoring program sometimes suppresses a danger alert. The customer cannot inspect the source. Explain why professional ethics is necessary here and give two purposes it serves.

Answer and marking points
  • The contractor has specialist knowledge of a risk that the customer cannot independently assess, creating a responsibility to disclose it.
  • Ethical standards protect people who might assume there is no danger when the warning is suppressed.
  • They make the contractor answerable for honest advice about whether the monitoring system can be trusted.

Common error: Apply the explanation to the hidden alert fault; unrelated benefits of automation do not answer this question.

Question 2 · Command word: Explain · 3 marks

A consultant recommends a supplier while secretly receiving a payment from that supplier. The consultant says, 'My employer has not forbidden this, so it must be ethical.' Explain why the reasoning is insufficient and suggest a responsible response.

Answer and marking points
  • An absent employer rule does not establish that the advice is independent or fair to the client.
  • The undisclosed payment creates a conflict between the consultant's personal interest and the client's needs.
  • The consultant should disclose the payment and arrange an impartial evaluation of the suppliers.

Common error: Explain the conflict rather than assuming that every recommendation involving payment is technically wrong.

Question 3 · Command word: Identify · 5 marks

Identify BCS and IEEE. A new engineer joins one of these bodies to obtain professional guidance. Explain two benefits of membership and one reason it cannot guarantee ethical behaviour.

Answer and marking points
  • BCS means British Computer Society, a professional computing body.
  • IEEE means Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers, a professional engineering body.
  • Its published conduct standards help the engineer decide how to raise a concern consistently with professional duties.
  • Its learning activities and peer network help the engineer develop competence and seek informed advice.
  • The engineer still chooses how to act and can disregard the standards despite being a member.

Common error: Explain the effects of membership rather than crediting membership as proof of good conduct.

04

Lesson review

Summary

Purpose

Professional decisions should protect affected people and sustain trust in specialist work.

Duties

Be competent, honest and accountable; disclose conflicts and protect confidential information.

Judgement

An instruction or a gap in the rules does not settle whether an action is responsible.

Membership

BCS and IEEE offer standards, development and guidance while members retain responsibility for their actions.
