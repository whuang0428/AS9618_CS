Professional ethics and professional bodies

Explain why computing professionals need ethical principles, apply their duties, and recognise how professional bodies support responsible practice.

What should a computing professional do when a requested action could harm users?

Before you start

An employee can read a customer record using a valid account. Does access permission alone justify every use of it?

Check your answer

No. Access permission does not establish that a use is appropriate; consider the purpose, affected people and professional responsibilities.

01

Why computing professionals need ethics

S7.01.A01

Visual overview

Connect professional power to dependence

Ethics concerns principles used to judge right and wrong conduct. Computing professionals can access private records, decide what a system does and explain whether its output can be trusted. These decisions affect people who may never meet the developer and may be unable to inspect the system themselves. A person affected by a decision is a stakeholder.

Explain why ethics is needed

If a developer alone knows that a warning sometimes fails, a customer cannot make an informed release decision unless that limitation is explained. The information imbalance is why the professional has a responsibility to give competent, honest advice. The duty does not disappear when a fault is difficult for others to discover.

Distinguish a purpose from a reputation claim

Ethical principles help professionals decide what to do when an instruction, deadline or personal interest conflicts with effects on others. Protecting people and their information is one purpose; making professionals answerable for their decisions is another. Trust should follow responsible conduct and evidence, rather than concealment that merely preserves a good appearance.

Connect to data protection

Lesson 033 distinguished security, privacy and integrity. A valid account may technically permit access to a record, while using that access for an unrelated personal purpose remains inappropriate. Technical permission answers whether the system allows an operation; ethics asks whether the professional should perform it in the circumstances.

Core explanation · Exam essentials

  • Specialist knowledge and control create responsibilities towards people affected by computing decisions.
  • Ethics guides responsible conduct, protects the public interest and supports justified trust and accountability.
Common misconception

A technically possible action is not automatically an ethical action.

Check your understanding

Why is ethics relevant even when a customer cannot detect an unsafe design?

Check your answer

The professional's responsibility to avoid foreseeable harm does not depend on whether the customer can discover the problem.

Professional duties and legal compliance

S7.01.A02

Visual overview

Turn duties into actions

Competence means having the knowledge and skills needed for the work, recognising limitations and obtaining qualified help when needed. Honesty includes stating uncertainty and known faults instead of overstating performance. Confidentiality restricts inappropriate disclosure. Integrity includes disclosing a personal interest that could distort professional advice. Each duty protects someone who depends on that work.

Separate the kinds of rule

Law imposes enforceable requirements; an employer sets work requirements; a professional code sets expected conduct. These can support the same action, but complying with one instruction does not answer every question. For example, a permitted data export may still be inappropriate for the proposed audience or purpose. Do not infer that an action is illegal solely because it is unethical.

Respond without creating an unrelated harm

When duties seem to conflict, establish the evidence, identify who needs it and seek an appropriate authorised review. A safety concern needs action, but publishing confidential customer records is not automatically the right way to report it. Record the facts and limitations, raise the issue through a suitable responsible person and propose a measure that addresses the specific risk.

Worked example · A failed warning test and an uncertain diagnosis

  1. Known conditions

    A new fire-warning interface failed to display a warning in one recorded test. A manager asks the developer to sign a report saying that all tests passed. The cause has not yet been confirmed.

  2. Duty and audience

    Occupants rely on warnings and the safety lead relies on the test report. The developer must report the failed result accurately and disclose the uncertainty about its cause.

  3. Immediate response

    Record the test conditions and actual result; notify the safety lead and recommend that the interface is not relied upon until the failure is investigated and corrected.

  4. Follow through

    After correction, rerun the relevant tests and record the outcomes. Passing those tests supports the specified test claims, not a promise that every possible failure has been ruled out.

  5. Change the evidence

    If there were only an unconfirmed report of a failure, describe it as a concern requiring investigation. Do not invent a passed test, claim a confirmed cause, or accuse a colleague without evidence.

Core explanation · Exam essentials

  • Work competently, report evidence honestly, protect confidentiality and disclose conflicts of interest.
  • An employer instruction or absence of an explicit prohibition does not settle an ethical decision.
Common misconception

Ethical reasoning requires an explanation of the duty and its consequences, not simply the label 'legal' or 'illegal'.

Check your understanding

A paid adviser recommends a supplier without revealing a personal commission. Must the recommended software be technically defective for an ethical concern to exist?

Check your answer

No. The undisclosed financial interest can compromise the independence of the advice even if the software works. Disclose the interest and arrange an impartial comparison against the client’s requirements.

BCS, IEEE and the value of membership

Identify the organisations and the code

BCS means British Computer Society; IEEE means Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers. They are professional organisations, not software suppliers or a programmer’s employer. They publish standards of professional conduct that members can use when explaining why a requested action is inappropriate.

Explain the purpose of a code

A code of conduct sets out expected behaviour and professional standards. It helps practitioners and the public understand what responsible work should look like and provides a basis for considering complaints or breaches. Associated conduct procedures provide accountability and possible consequences; the existence of a code alone does not prevent misconduct.

Explain why membership can help

Joining a body connects a practitioner with continuing professional development, technical information and experienced peers. Learning helps maintain competence; advice can help a new engineer recognise a risk or frame a concern. The ethical benefit comes from using that support in practice, rather than displaying the membership name.

Different questions about professional practice

QuestionAppropriate explanation
What is the code for?It states expected conduct and provides standards against which behaviour can be judged.
Why join the body?Use its learning, guidance and professional community to improve and support practice.
Who decides and remains responsible?The practitioner applies judgement; membership does not transfer responsibility to the body.
Is this product safe?Inspect design, testing and operating evidence; membership is not product certification.

Swipe horizontally to compare every column →

Apply the benefit to a difficulty

A programmer pressured to conceal a fault can explain the relevant honesty or public-interest duty using the code, then seek competent advice about reporting the risk. The body’s support and the employer’s release process play different roles. Do not promise that membership automatically supplies a particular service, removes liability or guarantees the outcome of a dispute.

Core explanation · Exam essentials

  • BCS and IEEE are professional bodies. Their codes state expected professional conduct and support accountability.
  • Membership can provide guidance, continuing development and peer support; it does not certify every member or product as ethical or safe.
Common misconception

Naming a professional body does not explain why joining it matters.

Check your understanding

Match these to their purpose: a conduct standard, a training event, and a completed product test. Does any one of them guarantee ethical behaviour?

Check your answer

The standard states expected behaviour; training supports competence; the test supplies evidence about the product under tested conditions. None guarantees every future action will be ethical or every product will be safe.

Question 1 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

Explain two reasons why professional ethics is needed when designing a system that stores private patient records.

S7.01.A01
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Patients depend on specialists to protect information they cannot directly control, so professional duties help prevent misuse of that access.
  2. Rules may not resolve every proposed use of the records, so ethical principles help the designer judge effects on patients.

Common error: Develop each reason using the records context.

Question 2 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

An employee is asked to sign off a system outside their expertise. Explain why obeying the instruction alone is insufficient and suggest a responsible action.

S7.01.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Approval without the necessary competence could give others unjustified confidence in the system.
  2. The employee should disclose the limit of their expertise and seek a suitably qualified review.

Common error: Link the action to the competence problem.

Question 3 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

Explain two ways joining a professional body could help a programmer keep their skills current and respond to pressure to hide a fault.

S7.02.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Continuing professional development can help the programmer learn current techniques and recognise limits in their knowledge.
  2. A professional code gives the programmer recognised standards with which to explain why the fault should be reported.

Common error: Connect each membership benefit to one of the two stated needs.

Original question · Cambridge 9618/13

1. Explain professional membership

Cambridge 9618/13 · October/November 2024 · 5(b) · 3 marks

Selected parts: 5(b) [3].

Reading the question — teacher guidance

Give reasons for a programmer to join a professional ethical body. Connect the support or standards to the programmer’s work.

Solution / model answer — teacher-written

The body supplies ethical guidelines so the programmer and clients know the standards expected of the work. It can provide training that keeps the programmer’s skills up to date. It can also offer professional support, including advice when ethical problems arise.

Official mark scheme
Why this earns marks — teacher explanation

The maximum is three, with one mark for each accepted point. The scheme also accepts reputation, entry requirements, recognition and legal advice; naming several bodies earns no extra credit.

Common mistakes — teacher analysis

“It is good for programmers” does not identify a benefit. A professional body is not the same as the programmer’s employer.

Teacher solution and explanation

Describe what the code establishes. You do not need to invent a disciplinary case or list an organisation’s rules.

A code of conduct sets out the behaviour and professional standards expected of members. It also explains the consequences of failing to follow those standards.

04

Lesson review

Summary

Purpose

Professional decisions should protect affected people and sustain trust in specialist work.

Duties

Be competent, honest and accountable; disclose conflicts and protect confidential information.

Judgement

An instruction or a gap in the rules does not settle whether an action is responsible.

Membership

BCS and IEEE offer standards, development and guidance while members retain responsibility for their actions.
