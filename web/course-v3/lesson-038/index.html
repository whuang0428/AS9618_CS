7.1.1

Professional ethical bodies

BCS · a professional body for computing

A professional body is an organisation supporting people in a profession and its standards. BCS brings computing practitioners together around competent, responsible work.

What its code means in practice

The BCS code covers the public interest, competence and integrity, duties to the relevant authority, and duties to the profession. A member still has to apply these principles to the particular situation.

Sources and scopeBCS Code of Conduct · organisation explanation; not an additional Cambridge marking rule.

IEEE · a professional body for engineering and technology

The code gives members a shared reference for decisions that affect colleagues, clients and the public. For example, an engineer should report the limitations of a system instead of claiming that it is reliable without evidence.

Teacher-written check

Does IEEE membership prove that a programmer’s software is safe?

Check your reasoning

No. Membership establishes professional expectations. The software still needs appropriate design, testing and review, and the programmer remains responsible for their decisions.

Sources and scopeIEEE Code of Ethics · IEEE Circuits and Systems Society

Why a computing professional needs ethics

Ethics concerns principles of right and wrong conduct. Computing professionals control systems and information that other people depend on. Ethical duties guide how they use that power, including when a deadline or an employer’s instruction conflicts with the public interest.

Turn professional duties into actions

Turn a duty into an action
DutyAction in professional work
CompetenceDisclose limits in your expertise and obtain qualified help.
Honesty and integrityReport actual results and disclose interests that could influence advice.
ConfidentialityShare sensitive information only with appropriate people for the agreed purpose.
AccountabilityBe able to explain and justify your decisions and their foreseeable effects.

Ethics, law and instructions

Law creates enforceable requirements. Ethical judgement also considers responsibilities and consequences. An instruction from a manager, or the absence of an explicit prohibition, does not make every action ethical.

Why join? · turn membership benefits into reasons

MS points explained · professional membership
Accepted ideaWhy it matters to the programmer
Ethical guidelines and standardsProvide a reference for deciding what is expected; clients and colleagues know the standards that should apply.
Professional recognition and reputationMembership requirements and recognition can support confidence in the programmer’s professional standing.
Professional help or adviceProvide support when the programmer encounters a difficult professional problem.
Training and updating skillsHelp the programmer keep knowledge and competence current as technology changes.

Build an explanation

Example: “The body provides training, helping the programmer keep their skills up to date.” State a distinct benefit and connect it to professional work. Merely naming BCS and IEEE does not explain why membership matters.

Past paper · explain professional membership

Original question · Cambridge 9618/13

1. Explain professional membership

Cambridge 9618/13 · October/November 2024 · 5(b) · 3 marks

Selected parts: 5(b) [3].

Original question
Reading the question — teacher guidance

Give reasons for a programmer to join a professional ethical body. Connect the support or standards to the programmer’s work.

Solution / model answer — teacher-written

The body supplies ethical guidelines so the programmer and clients know the standards expected of the work. It can provide training that keeps the programmer’s skills up to date. It can also offer professional support, including advice when ethical problems arise.

Official mark scheme
Why this earns marks — teacher explanation

The maximum is three, with one mark for each accepted point. The scheme also accepts reputation, entry requirements, recognition and legal advice; naming several bodies earns no extra credit.

Common mistakes — teacher analysis

“It is good for programmers” does not identify a benefit. A professional body is not the same as the programmer’s employer.

What is the purpose of a code of conduct?

The code makes professional standards explicit. It helps a programmer explain why concealing a known fault is unacceptable, even when a manager wants an immediate release. Training develops the programmer’s skills; the code establishes expectations for how those skills are used.

Sources and scope9618/12/O/N/25 · Q2(b) · 2 marks. The MS accepts distinct purposes such as expected behaviour, consequences, good practice, safe professional work and public trust. Two accepted points are enough for this question.

Teacher-written check

Give one purpose of a code and one benefit of membership. Why are these not the same answer?

Check your reasoning

A code establishes the behaviour expected of members. Membership can provide training to update technical skills. The first explains a professional standard; the second explains support available to the member.

Original question · Cambridge 9618/12

2. State the purpose of a code of conduct

Cambridge 9618/12 · October/November 2025 · 2(b) · 2 marks

Selected parts: 2(b) [2].

Original question
Reading the question — teacher guidance

Describe what the code establishes. You do not need to invent a disciplinary case or list an organisation’s rules.

Solution / model answer — teacher-written

A code of conduct sets out the behaviour and professional standards expected of members. It also explains the consequences of failing to follow those standards.

Official mark scheme
Why this earns marks — teacher explanation

Two accepted purpose statements earn the two available marks. The scheme also accepts promoting safe professional work, good practice and public trust.

Common mistakes — teacher analysis

A code of conduct guides professional behaviour; program source code instructs a computer.

Teacher-written practice
Question 1 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

Explain two reasons why professional ethics is needed when designing a system that stores private patient records.

S7.01.A01
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Patients depend on specialists to protect information they cannot directly control, so professional duties help prevent misuse of that access.
  2. Rules may not resolve every proposed use of the records, so ethical principles help the designer judge effects on patients.

Common error: Develop each reason using the records context.

Question 2 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

An employee is asked to sign off a system outside their expertise. Explain why obeying the instruction alone is insufficient and suggest a responsible action.

S7.01.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Approval without the necessary competence could give others unjustified confidence in the system.
  2. The employee should disclose the limit of their expertise and seek a suitably qualified review.

Common error: Link the action to the competence problem.

Question 3 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

Explain two ways joining a professional body could help a programmer keep their skills current and respond to pressure to hide a fault.

S7.02.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Continuing professional development can help the programmer learn current techniques and recognise limits in their knowledge.
  2. A professional code gives the programmer recognised standards with which to explain why the fault should be reported.

Common error: Connect each membership benefit to one of the two stated needs.

Question 4 · Command word: State2 teacher marks

State two purposes of ethics in the computing profession.

S7.01.A01
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Protect the public interest when computing decisions affect people.
  2. Promote trustworthy, accountable professional conduct.

Common error: Two restatements of 'doing the right thing' are not two distinct purposes.

Question 5 · Command word: Identify2 teacher marks

Identify the professional bodies abbreviated BCS and IEEE.

S7.02.A01
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. BCS: British Computer Society.
  2. IEEE: Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers.

Common error: Give the organisation names rather than examples of software companies.
