Artificial intelligence

What makes this an AI task?

Artificial intelligence (AI)

Artificial intelligence enables computer systems to perform tasks associated with human intelligence, including recognising patterns, interpreting language, reasoning and predicting outcomes. It may be entirely software: a physical robot is not required.

Teacher-written check

A machine adds two entered prices. Another recognises an item from its photograph. Which description demonstrates an AI task?

Check your reasoning

The second describes image recognition: the program infers the object from visual patterns. Adding already supplied numbers alone does not establish AI. Recognition can still be wrong.

Where does the system’s knowledge come from?

Keep the distinction

A rule-based diagnostic system might infer a likely fault from symptoms using expert-written rules. In machine learning, a training procedure instead adjusts a model from data or experience. Neither approach guarantees that its conclusion is correct.

What is an artificial neural network?

Keep the distinction

An artificial neural network processes information through connected layers. Training changes numerical values, such as connection weights, so outputs better fit the learning objective. Deep learning uses networks with multiple hidden layers; it does not mean that the computer understands or thinks like a person.

Teacher-written check

Does using a deep neural network make a prediction certain?

Check your reasoning

No. Model structure does not guarantee a correct output. The data, training, evaluation and new operating conditions still matter.

Supervised learning · examples include target answers

Keep the distinction

Supervised learning uses examples with target answers to learn an input-to-output relationship. The target can be a category, such as a character, or a numerical value. “Supervised” does not require a person to watch every training calculation.

Teacher-written check

A training record contains a pump’s vibration readings and whether it later failed. What is the label?

Check your reasoning

Whether the pump later failed is the known target answer. The vibration readings are input features used to predict that target.

Unsupervised learning · find structure without target labels

Keep the distinction

Unsupervised learning looks for structure in data without supplied target answers for the task. Clustering groups similar records. A person can interpret a group after it has been found; the group’s meaning was not supplied as a correct training label.

Check your reasoning

Reinforcement learning · actions lead to feedback

Keep the distinction

Reinforcement learning develops a strategy through actions and feedback from an environment. A reward evaluates an outcome; it need not tell the learner the correct action to take. Rewards may arrive after a sequence of actions. This is still training.

Teacher-written check

Is a reward of +10 the same as a label saying “move left”?

Check your reasoning

No. It evaluates what happened but does not directly supply the correct action for every situation. The learner must work out which action choices tend to achieve higher cumulative reward.

1. Connect classroom AI monitoring to an ethical impact

Cambridge 9618/13 · October/November 2025 · 7(a) · 2 marks

Selected parts: 7(a) [2].

Original question
Reading the question — teacher guidance

Describe one impact of the stated classroom monitoring. Name the affected people and explain how recording or analysing their behaviour could affect them. One developed impact is sufficient; the question does not require both a benefit and a drawback.

Solution / model answer — teacher-written

Continuous classroom monitoring can raise privacy concerns: students or teachers may feel uncomfortable because their actions are constantly observed and may be passed to third parties.

Official mark scheme
Why this earns marks — teacher explanation

The official scheme awards one mark per accepted point, up to two. Privacy concerns linked to constant surveillance or sharing data are accepted. It also accepts improved learning through identifying students who need support, or pressure from monitoring leading to stress or anxiety. A suitable positive impact can therefore earn credit.

This task assesses a specific social or ethical impact of AI. It does not require a technical explanation of image recognition, a calculation, or a complete comparison of two possible deployment decisions.

Common mistakes — teacher analysis

Writing only that AI is unethical does not describe its impact. Explain what happens to students or teachers; the next part of the original paper concerns video file size and is outside this selection.

Original question · Cambridge 9618/11

2. Describe two social impacts of AI homework help

Cambridge 9618/11 · October/November 2025 · 8(c) · 4 marks

Selected parts: 8(c) [4].

Original question
Reading the question — teacher guidance

Answer part 8(c). Describe two social impacts on students and develop each in the homework context. The network-ethics part is taught separately in 7.1.2.

Solution / model answer — teacher-written

Learning support: additional AI help may improve achievement; students who struggle in traditional lessons may also gain confidence from learning at their own pace.

Social participation: heavy reliance on AI may reduce collaboration and face-to-face communication; increased screen time and isolation can contribute to anxiety or loneliness.

Official mark scheme
Why this earns marks — teacher explanation

The scheme awards one mark per accepted bullet point, up to four overall. There is no stated two-mark cap per impact and no requirement to give one benefit and one drawback. Other accepted points include reduced reasoning practice, unequal access, incorrect answers, intellectual-property concerns and changes to school assessment.

Common mistakes — teacher analysis

Describe two developed impacts, not a disconnected list of labels. Do not claim that all AI answers are wrong or that all students become isolated.

Teacher-written check

A business saves two hours of checking each day. Why is that not enough to prove that AI saves money?

Check your reasoning

The value of the saved work must be compared with the cost of the AI service, integration, maintenance, oversight and correcting errors. Whether staff expenditure falls also depends on how the saved time is used.

Define what is being compared

Lower consumption in the controlled equipment is only one side of the comparison. The AI service and additional hardware also have impacts. Electricity use, emissions, water use and waste are different quantities: evidence about one does not settle all the others.

Application · turn a photographed label into translated speech

Name what each stage changes

Optical character recognition (OCR) locates characters in an image and converts their pixel patterns into individual characters. Natural language processing (NLP) combines the characters into words and compares them with a database to identify words in the source language. Words are then matched to the selected language for translation. Text-to-speech generates audio waveforms corresponding to the words. Speech-to-text takes audio as input and is a different direction.

Sources and scope9618/12/M/J/25 · Q3(a), 4 marks · recognised characters, language processing/translation and speech generation. The camera and speaker show the full flow; each displayed box is not automatically a separate mark.

Teacher-written check

A clear voice speaks the wrong word after OCR misreads the label. Would increasing the volume fix the fault?

Check your reasoning

No. Clear audio can carry an earlier recognition error forward. Check the recognised text against the label or capture a clearer image before translating and speaking it.

Original question
Reading the question — teacher guidance

Follow the product’s sequence: camera image, recognised words, translation, spoken output. For social benefits, identify people helped and what they can do more easily.

Solution / model answer — teacher-written

3(a): Optical character recognition locates characters in the camera image and converts their pixel patterns into individual characters. Teacher-written practice
Question 1 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

Explain what is meant by artificial intelligence and distinguish recognising a handwritten postcode from storing a postcode typed by a user.

S7.06.A01
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. AI performs tasks associated with human intelligence, such as interpreting patterns or making inferences.
  2. Handwriting recognition interprets an image to infer characters, while storing an already typed postcode alone does not demonstrate that task.

Common error: Do not define AI as all uses of computers or only as robots.

Question 2 · Command word: Describe3 teacher marks

An AI payment service flags unusual transactions for staff review. Describe what the model analyses and how its output is used; explain why a flag is not proof of fraud.

S7.06.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. The model examines features of a new payment in relation to transaction patterns.
  2. A risk flag directs staff attention to a payment needing investigation.
  3. A legitimate unusual payment can also be flagged, so further evidence is needed.

Common error: A prediction or classification may be wrong.

Question 3 · Command word: Explain3 teacher marks

Automatic captions work well for one accent but often misrecognise another. Explain one social benefit and one social risk of using them in an online lesson, and suggest a safeguard.

S7.06.A03
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Captions can give students who cannot hear the audio access to the spoken lesson.
  2. Students relying on poorly recognised speech may receive inaccurate information and unequal access.
  3. Provide a way to correct captions or supply checked lesson text for affected users.

Common error: Name the students affected and connect the safeguard to caption errors.

Question 4 · Command word: Explain3 teacher marks

A factory uses an AI system that forecasts machine faults. Explain one economic benefit, one system cost and one possible effect on workers.

S7.06.A04
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Earlier repair scheduling can reduce production time lost to unexpected breakdowns.
  2. Sensors, model operation and integration require expenditure even when forecasts are useful.
  3. Maintenance staff may need retraining to interpret alerts and plan work differently.

Common error: Do not treat a reduction in emergency repairs as elimination of all maintenance jobs.

Question 5 · Command word: Evaluate4 teacher marks

A recruitment firm wants AI to reject applicants automatically. Testing shows good overall accuracy but much poorer results for one group. Evaluate automatic rejection and recommend a safeguard.

S7.06.A03S7.06.A06
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Automated screening can process applications quickly, reducing routine staff workload.
  2. The poorer group performance can unfairly exclude suitable applicants, so the overall average is insufficient evidence of fairness.
  3. Use audited assistance with human review and an appeal route rather than final rejection solely from the model.
  4. Deployment should depend on investigating the unequal errors and showing that review can address them.

Common error: A judgement must use the supplied group-performance evidence.

Question 6 · Command word: Calculate3 teacher marks

A delivery company compares two routing systems over the same month, with the same deliveries. The previous routes use 6000 kWh-equivalent of fuel energy. New routes use 5700, while the AI service adds 200 kWh of operating electricity, including cooling. Calculate the change in the stated operating-energy total. Then calculate it if the service instead adds 400 kWh, and explain why neither calculation alone proves an overall environmental improvement.

S7.06.A05S7.06.A06
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. With 200 kWh additional operation, the new total is 5700 + 200 = 5900 kWh-equivalent, 100 less than the supplied 6000 baseline.
  2. With 400 kWh additional operation, the new total is 6100 kWh-equivalent, 100 more than the baseline despite shorter-route fuel savings.
  3. This is the supplied final-energy accounting comparison only. Fuel and electricity can have different emissions per unit; hardware manufacture, lifetime and disposal and other resource impacts are also uncounted, so a broader environmental claim needs further evidence.

Common error: Do not count cooling twice or treat equal energy quantities from different sources as necessarily equal emissions.

Question 7 · Command word: Describe5 teacher marks

A museum device photographs a printed Italian description, translates it into English and reads it aloud. Describe the role and intermediate output of OCR, language processing, translation and text-to-speech. A blurred image causes a wrong recognised word that is then spoken clearly: explain one suitable correction and why clear speech does not establish a correct reading.

S7.06.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. OCR locates character patterns in the camera image and converts them to text; pixels are not already a recognised character sequence.
  2. Natural language processing combines the recognised characters into words and compares the words with a database to identify matches in the source language; it may predict incomplete words but cannot guarantee repair of every recognition error.
  3. Translation compares the words with a database of the chosen language to find matching English words.
  4. Text-to-speech generates the corresponding audio waveform for playback through the speaker.
  5. Retake the blurred image or verify and correct the recognised text before relying on the translation. Clear speech can reproduce an incorrect intermediate word, so it does not prove correct recognition.

Common error: The camera supplies images; speech-to-text is not the conversion from translated text to audio.

S7.06.A02

  1. Recorded or live speech supplies audio input.
  2. The system recognises speech patterns and infers the spoken words.
  3. It produces text that can be used for captions or dictation.

Question 9 · Command word: Explain3 teacher marks

An AI system reduces a building's heating demand but requires new servers. Explain one environmental benefit and two environmental costs that should be considered.

S7.06.A05
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Lower heating demand can reduce fuel or electricity consumption in the building.
  2. Running and cooling the servers consumes energy and may add emissions.

Question 10 · Command word: Calculate4 teacher marks

In an invented caption test, group A has 160 correct captions out of 200 and group B has 20 correct captions out of 50. Each caption is checked against a reference and counted as a whole correct or incorrect item. Calculate the overall correct percentage and each group’s percentage, then explain one limitation of using the overall figure to approve deployment.

S7.06.A03S7.06.A06
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. The overall rate is (160 + 20) / (200 + 50) × 100 = 72%.
  2. Group A has 160 / 200 × 100 = 80% correct.
  3. Group B has 20 / 50 × 100 = 40% correct.
  4. The overall rate weights the larger group more heavily and hides B’s much poorer access. Check representative group performance and workable correction before deployment; the discrepancy alone does not prove its cause.

Common error: Do not average 80% and 40% without weighting by the different numbers of tested captions.
