Lesson 041 · S7-L04 · S7.06

Artificial intelligence applications and impacts

Explain what AI does, connect applications to their decisions and evaluate social, economic and environmental consequences in context.

How does an AI system's particular decision create benefits, risks and costs?

Before you start

A warehouse replaces paper forms with an electronic list. Does that change alone establish that it uses AI?

Check your answer

No. Storing information electronically does not by itself demonstrate an AI task such as recognition, inference or prediction.

Teaching checkpoints
  • Answer each knowledge-unit check before revealing its explanation.
  • Use the worked scenario to connect a claim to evidence, then attempt the practice and independent exam questions.

What artificial intelligence does

S7.06.A01

Core explanation

Artificial intelligence (AI) enables computer systems to perform tasks associated with human intelligence, such as interpreting language, recognising patterns, reasoning or making predictions and decisions. A system may use expert-written rules or a model learned from data to infer an output for a new input.

An AI system does not have to be a physical robot, conscious or human-like in every respect. A recommendation service can use AI entirely in software. Conversely, recording information or performing a fixed arithmetic calculation does not by itself demonstrate AI. Identify the interpretation, inference or decision task being performed.

Common misconception

AI is not synonymous with every automated system, and it does not guarantee human understanding.

Check your understanding

Can a recommendation website use AI without a robot?

Check your answer

Yes. A software model can infer suitable recommendations from patterns in data without controlling a physical machine.

02

From input to an AI-assisted action

S7.06.A02

Visual overview

Core explanation

Describe an application as a connected chain. State the input information, what the AI infers or recognises, the resulting output and how that output is used. 'AI is used in medicine' names an area but does not explain an application; interpreting a scan to flag a possible abnormality gives a task and a use.

Outputs can guide a person or control an action. A fraud-detection model may flag a payment for an investigator, while an autonomous controller may select an action from sensor readings. Confidence, reliability and consequences determine how much checking or human intervention is appropriate. A prediction can be useful without being certain.

Worked example · A pump maintenance alert

  1. Input

    A factory records vibration and temperature from a water pump and has examples of earlier faults.

  2. Inference

    The model recognises a pattern associated with bearing wear and estimates an increased chance of failure.

  3. Use

    A technician inspects the bearing and schedules a repair before an unexpected shutdown.

  4. Limit

    A false alert could prompt unnecessary work, while a missed fault could still cause a breakdown; the prediction needs validation and monitoring.

Common misconception

A list of sectors is not a description of what an AI system does in those sectors.

Check your understanding

In speech-to-text, what is the input and what does the AI output?

Check your answer

Audio speech is the input; recognised words represented as text are the output.

03

Social effects: access, fairness and privacy

S7.06.A03

Visual overview

Core explanation

Social impacts concern how people live, participate and are treated. Speech recognition can improve access for someone unable to type, and translation can help people communicate. The benefit depends on whether the system works adequately for its users, including different accents, languages or disabilities.

Biased or unrepresentative data can contribute to unequal error rates and unfair decisions. Collecting personal information also creates privacy risks. Explain who benefits or is harmed: a hiring system that rejects qualified applicants unfairly changes their opportunities. Testing across affected groups, limiting unnecessary collection and providing human review or appeal can reduce specific risks.

Common misconception

Consistent automated processing can consistently repeat a bias; consistency is not proof of fairness.

Check your understanding

Why might an average accuracy figure hide an accessibility problem?

Check your answer

Good performance for a large group can conceal much worse performance for a smaller group with different speech or access needs.

Core explanation

Economic impacts concern resources, production, costs and employment. An AI tool may process routine cases more quickly, reduce waste or predict failures before expensive disruption. Link the saving to the activity: an early equipment warning may allow planned maintenance during a quiet period rather than an emergency shutdown.

Costs include developing or buying the system, obtaining and maintaining suitable data, operating hardware and training staff. Incorrect decisions can create rework or losses. Automation may reduce demand for some tasks while creating or changing roles in supervision, maintenance and specialist support; workers may need retraining. A productivity gain does not mean every worker or business benefits equally.

Common misconception

AI does not necessarily remove all jobs or eliminate operating costs.

Check your understanding

Why is fewer manual checks not enough to prove a net saving?

Check your answer

Compare the labour saving with purchase, operation, oversight, retraining and error costs for the actual deployment.

05

Environmental costs and potential savings

S7.06.A05

Visual overview

Core explanation

Training and operating AI models uses computing equipment and electricity, and cooling may require water or additional energy. Manufacturing and replacing hardware also uses materials and produces waste. Emissions depend partly on the energy supply, so identical computation can have different environmental consequences in different settings.

An AI application can also reduce resource use: optimising delivery routes may reduce fuel consumption, adjusting heating to occupancy may reduce energy waste, and detecting faults may prolong equipment life. Compare these savings with the system's own operation and hardware lifecycle. The result depends on the scale of use and the alternative being replaced, not on an assumption that all AI is environmentally beneficial or harmful.

Common misconception

Lower energy use in the controlled device is not the whole environmental balance if the AI service itself consumes resources.

Check your understanding

What comparison would help assess an AI route planner's environmental benefit?

Check your answer

Compare reduced vehicle fuel use with the planner's computing and hardware footprint, using the previous routing method as the baseline.

06

Evaluate an application in its setting

S7.06.A06

Visual overview

Evaluate a proposed deployment

  1. ApplicationIdentify the input, inference and decision affected.
  2. ImpactsDevelop benefits and risks using the relevant setting.
  3. ControlMatch safeguards or alternatives to the specific risks.
  4. JudgementRecommend a course with reasons, conditions and remaining limits.

Core explanation

Start an evaluation by identifying the AI task and the decision it influences. Develop relevant benefits and drawbacks through effects on named stakeholders or resources. Consider social, economic and environmental dimensions where they apply to the question, but do not force an unrelated point into a response simply to repeat three labels.

Reach a supported judgement using the supplied constraints. State a suitable safeguard or alternative and explain the risk it addresses. Human oversight needs a workable review process and competent reviewers; writing 'a human checks it' does not establish that errors will be caught. A conditional recommendation should say which evidence or conditions would make deployment acceptable.

Worked example · A hospital tests scan prioritisation

  1. Proposal

    An AI service flags scans that may need urgent review. Clinicians retain responsibility for interpreting scans, and the hospital is comparing it with its existing queue.

  2. Benefits

    Faster flagging may shorten waits for urgent cases and let specialists direct their time to patients needing rapid attention.

  3. Costs and risks

    Missed cases could delay care, different patient groups may experience different error rates, and the service adds operating and equipment costs.

  4. Safeguards

    Evaluate sensitivity across patient groups, retain a route for reviewing unflagged scans and allow clinicians to override the queue priority.

  5. Judgement

    Use a monitored pilot if it improves waiting times without unacceptable missed cases. Compare its resource costs with the existing process before expanding it.

Common misconception

A general claim that AI is fast, accurate or objective is not a context-based evaluation.

Check your understanding

What makes 'Use AI if it is safe' an incomplete conclusion?

Check your answer

It does not specify how safety will be assessed, what safeguards are needed or how the decision follows from the scenario's evidence.

Cambridge command words

Practice questions

Question 1 · Command word: Explain2 marks

Explain what is meant by artificial intelligence and distinguish recognising a handwritten postcode from storing a postcode typed by a user.

S7.06.A01
Answer and marking points
  1. AI performs tasks associated with human intelligence, such as interpreting patterns or making inferences.
  2. Handwriting recognition interprets an image to infer characters, while storing an already typed postcode alone does not demonstrate that task.

Common error: Do not define AI as all uses of computers or only as robots.

Question 2 · Command word: Describe3 marks

Describe an AI speech-to-text application using its input, recognition task, output and practical use.

S7.06.A02
Answer and marking points
  1. Recorded or live speech supplies audio input.
  2. The system recognises speech patterns and infers the spoken words.
  3. It produces text that can be used for captions or dictation.

Common error: Connect the stages instead of listing unrelated AI application areas.

Question 3 · Command word: Describe3 marks

An AI payment service flags unusual transactions for staff review. Describe what the model analyses and how its output is used; explain why a flag is not proof of fraud.

S7.06.A02
Answer and marking points
  1. The model examines features of a new payment in relation to transaction patterns.
  2. A risk flag directs staff attention to a payment needing investigation.
  3. A legitimate unusual payment can also be flagged, so further evidence is needed.

Common error: A prediction or classification may be wrong.

Question 4 · Command word: Explain3 marks

Automatic captions work well for one accent but often misrecognise another. Explain one social benefit and one social risk of using them in an online lesson, and suggest a safeguard.

S7.06.A03
Answer and marking points
  1. Captions can give students who cannot hear the audio access to the spoken lesson.
  2. Students relying on poorly recognised speech may receive inaccurate information and unequal access.
  3. Provide a way to correct captions or supply checked lesson text for affected users.

Common error: Name the students affected and connect the safeguard to caption errors.

Question 5 · Command word: Predict3 marks

A factory uses AI to predict machine faults. Explain one economic benefit, one system cost and one possible effect on workers.

S7.06.A04
Answer and marking points
  1. Earlier repair scheduling can reduce production time lost to unexpected breakdowns.
  2. Sensors, model operation and integration require expenditure even when forecasts are useful.
  3. Maintenance staff may need retraining to interpret alerts and plan work differently.

Common error: Do not treat a reduction in emergency repairs as elimination of all maintenance jobs.

Question 6 · Command word: Explain3 marks

An AI system reduces a building's heating demand but requires new servers. Explain one environmental benefit and two environmental costs that should be considered.

S7.06.A05
Answer and marking points
  1. Lower heating demand can reduce fuel or electricity consumption in the building.
  2. Running and cooling the servers consumes energy and may add emissions.
  3. Making and eventually replacing the servers uses materials and creates disposal impacts.

Common error: Include the AI equipment's footprint as well as the controlled building's saving.

Question 7 · Command word: Evaluate4 marks

A recruitment firm wants AI to reject applicants automatically. Testing shows good overall accuracy but much poorer results for one group. Evaluate automatic rejection and recommend a safeguard.

S7.06.A03S7.06.A06
Answer and marking points
  1. Automated screening can process applications quickly, reducing routine staff workload.
  2. The poorer group performance can unfairly exclude suitable applicants, so the overall average is insufficient evidence of fairness.
  3. Use audited assistance with human review and an appeal route rather than final rejection solely from the model.
  4. Deployment should depend on investigating the unequal errors and showing that review can address them.

Common error: A judgement must use the supplied group-performance evidence.

Question 8 · Command word: Explain2 marks

A delivery company claims its AI route planner is environmentally beneficial because its computers are efficient. Explain why this evidence alone is insufficient and suggest a more useful comparison.

S7.06.A05S7.06.A06
Answer and marking points
  1. Efficient computers still consume resources and do not establish that the resulting vehicle routes save fuel.
  2. Compare total vehicle fuel use and the planning system's energy and hardware impacts with those of the previous routing approach.

Common error: State a baseline and evaluate the whole application rather than one component.

Question 1 · Command word: Explain · 4 marks

A recycling centre uses camera images to recognise material types and direct items to different bins. Explain why the recognition is an AI application, describe the input-to-action chain and explain one consequence of an incorrect classification.

Answer and marking points
  • The system interprets visual patterns to infer a material category, a task associated with intelligent recognition.
  • A camera supplies images of items on the sorting belt.
  • A model assigns a category and the controller directs the item towards its corresponding bin.
  • A mistaken category may contaminate a recyclable-material stream or send a reusable item to disposal.

Common error: Describe recognition rather than claiming that moving an item alone proves AI is present.

Question 2 · Command word: Evaluate · 5 marks

A council proposes AI to prioritise requests for housing repairs. It expects faster processing and lower administration costs, but tests show that requests written in less common languages are often assigned low priority incorrectly. Evaluate the proposal, including a social effect, an economic effect, a safeguard and a justified conclusion.

Answer and marking points
  • Faster triage could shorten delays before urgent repairs reach the appropriate team.
  • The language-related errors could leave some residents waiting longer for equally serious faults, creating unequal treatment.
  • Less routine administration may save staff time, although translation, integration and oversight still have costs.
  • Arrange multilingual review and an accessible challenge route for low-priority decisions to address the observed error pattern.
  • Use the system only as monitored assistance after checking that the review process corrects these errors; the given tests do not justify unreviewed prioritisation.

Common error: Credit a different supported judgement if it addresses the unequal errors, costs and available controls.

Question 3 · Command word: Explain · 5 marks

A farm is considering AI irrigation. Sensors feed a model that predicts when crops need water. Explain two potential environmental benefits and two environmental costs, then state what evidence is needed before claiming a net environmental improvement.

Answer and marking points
  • More targeted watering may reduce extraction of scarce water compared with the existing schedule.
  • Avoiding unnecessary pumping may lower the energy used to irrigate the fields.
  • Model operation and data transmission require electricity throughout the season.
  • Manufacturing and replacing sensors and computing devices consumes resources and adds waste.
  • Compare measured water and energy savings with the system's operating and hardware impacts over its life against the existing irrigation method.

Common error: The ability to predict watering needs does not by itself prove a net resource saving.

Summary

Meaning and application

Identify the interpretation or inference task, then connect input, output and the decision affected.

Social

Consider access, privacy and fair treatment for the actual users.

Economic

Weigh productivity and avoided losses against costs and changes to work.

Environmental

Compare application savings with computation and hardware over their lifecycle.

Evaluation

Use scenario evidence to justify a decision with specific safeguards and limits.
