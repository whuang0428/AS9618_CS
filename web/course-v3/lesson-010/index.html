Communicate data · Concept 05

Carry the same bits using electrical signals or light

Observe

Two cables for a school connection

  • A copper cable carries changing electrical signals.
  • A fibre-optic cable carries light pulses.
  • Both carry an encoded representation of the data.

Look, then discuss

If a file moves from a copper link to a fibre link, must the meaning of the file change?

Discuss the observation

The carrier can change while the represented data remains the same. Equipment converts between the link’s physical signals and the data it processes.

Recall

Separate a bit from its physical signal

A bit is a binary value. Electrical states, pulses of light or controlled radio-wave properties can represent data. Real signalling schemes can encode several bits per symbol; this course does not assume that every technology uses one visible pulse for one bit.

Revisit: Locate the processing, software and working data

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 4

Use copper for an electrical path

Copper conductors carry electrical signals. Twisted-pair cable twists pairs of wires to help reduce interference; coaxial cable uses a central conductor and surrounding shielding. Cable type, length and installation affect the signal and the achievable service.

Explanation · 2 / 4

Use fibre for an optical path

Fibre carries light through a glass or plastic core. An optical transmitter creates the signal and a receiver detects it. Fibre avoids electromagnetic interference in the transmission medium and can support high-capacity links over suitable distances, with appropriate equipment.

Explanation · 3 / 4

Explain practical trade-offs

Copper can be convenient and economical for many short fixed-device connections. Fibre can suit building-to-building links and electrically noisy routes, but installation, termination and optical equipment affect cost and maintenance. Compare the actual requirement, not an unsupported claim that every fibre service is faster than every copper one.

Explanation · 4 / 4

Notice the wired constraint

A wired endpoint needs a physical cable path and connection point. That is useful for a fixed desktop but restricts movement. A cable still needs suitable equipment at both ends; a length of cable by itself does not provide a network service.

Work together

Connect fixed desks and a distant building

The school needs short links from desks to classroom equipment and a longer route to a building beside electrically noisy machinery.

Work together · 1 / 3

Choose the desk link

Suitable copper cable can meet the short fixed-link requirement with available equipment at reasonable installation cost.

Work together · 2 / 3

Choose the inter-building carrier

Fibre is a strong candidate because its optical medium is unaffected by electromagnetic interference and suitable equipment can support the required distance and capacity.

Work together · 3 / 3

Keep the decision conditional

Check cable specifications, route length, required data rate and total equipment cost. The material name alone does not establish the full service.

Communicate data · Concept 06

Connect a moving device through radio coverage

Observe

Carry a tablet from the front to the back of the classroom

  • The tablet has no trailing network cable.
  • The signal must still reach network equipment.
  • Walls and other devices can affect the usable radio link.

Look, then discuss

Why can a wireless device lose service without anyone unplugging a cable?

Discuss the observation

Wireless does not mean a connection without physical conditions. The data travels using electromagnetic waves, and successful reception depends on the environment and equipment.

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 4

Represent data with radio waves

Wireless radio communication transmits an encoded signal through space. An antenna sends or receives electromagnetic waves. WiFi is a family of technologies used for wireless local networking; it is not another name for the internet.

Explanation · 2 / 4

Explain mobility

A tablet can move within usable wireless coverage without maintaining a cable to the tablet. This supports flexible classroom seating and portable devices. Coverage still has limits, and movement can change signal conditions.

Explanation · 3 / 4

Explain shared conditions

Obstacles, distance, interference and competing devices can reduce the usable service. Multiple users may share the available wireless capacity. More users do not create more total capacity merely by joining the network.

Explanation · 4 / 4

Locate the remaining infrastructure

A wireless access point connects wireless devices to the local network. It still needs power and an onward connection, often a cable. Wireless access needs authentication and appropriate protection; radio coverage alone is not permission to use school resources.

Work together

Explain a connected tablet that cannot load a page

A tablet is connected to the school WiFi. The school’s external connection fails while the local timetable server remains reachable.

Work together · 1 / 3

Locate the working part

The tablet’s radio link to local network equipment can remain active.

Work together · 2 / 3

Locate the failed part

An external page may be unreachable because the onward internet connection is unavailable.

Work together · 3 / 3

Test the distinction

A reachable local timetable would show that a working WiFi link does not guarantee internet access, and failed internet access does not prove the radio link failed.

Separate local wireless access from the services reached through it.

Communicate data · Concept 07

Join two fixed locations with a directional radio path

Observe

Two school buildings with a road between them

  • Running a new cable under the road is difficult.
  • Rooftop antennas can face each other.
  • A taller new building could obstruct the route.

Look, then discuss

Why does the position of an obstacle matter to this link?

Discuss the observation

The proposed link depends on a directed radio path between fixed antennas. The installation needs a suitable clear path rather than merely two devices that support wireless communication.

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 3

Place microwaves within radio communication

Microwaves are electromagnetic waves in a high-frequency part of the radio spectrum. They are used in several communication systems. Here, consider a terrestrial point-to-point microwave link between directional antennas.

Explanation · 2 / 3

Explain the directional path

The antennas direct the signal between endpoints. Such links commonly need a suitable line-of-sight path, appropriate antenna height and alignment. An obstruction can weaken or block the path, so the physical route must be checked.

Explanation · 3 / 3

Connect the benefit to installation

A point-to-point wireless link can cross a route where installing cable is difficult. Equipment, antenna positioning, permitted operation and environmental conditions still constrain the service. Weather effects depend on the frequency and design; do not promise that every microwave link behaves identically.

Work together

Evaluate the rooftop proposal

The school can mount two antennas with a clear path today, but a planned extension may stand between them.

Work together · 1 / 3

Identify the benefit

The link can join the buildings without laying a cable across the road.

Work together · 2 / 3

Test the dependency

Check whether the planned extension obstructs the required path. The current clear view is not enough to guarantee future operation.

Work together · 3 / 3

Respond to the changed condition

A different antenna location or another route may be needed. Merely increasing the number of classroom computers does not remove the obstruction.

A directional link is a physical route with conditions, not an abstract arrow.

Predict the main installation advantage and the path constraint. Compare with a fibre route across the same site.

Communicate data · Concept 08

Follow the uplink, relay and downlink

Observe

Connect a school expedition station

  • The station is beyond available fixed-line access.
  • Ground equipment communicates with a satellite.
  • The satellite relays the communication towards another ground endpoint.

Look, then discuss

Why can the connection reach a remote place yet still introduce a noticeable delay?

Discuss the observation

Coverage and delay describe different properties. A relay can extend reach while adding signal travel distance and processing or network delays.

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 4

Name the two directions

The ground-to-satellite transmission is an uplink. The satellite-to-ground transmission is a downlink. Suitable ground antennas and equipment send and receive the radio signals; a satellite is a relay in the communication path.

Explanation · 2 / 4

Explain the coverage benefit

Satellite services can connect locations where laying cable or reaching nearby terrestrial infrastructure is difficult. Availability depends on the service coverage, ground equipment and a usable view or radio path to the satellite system.

Explanation · 3 / 4

Explain delay without confusing it with capacity

Signals take time to travel. A longer route adds propagation delay even when the link can carry many bits per second. Satellite altitude and system design matter: do not assign every satellite service the same delay or assume all satellites remain fixed above one place.

Explanation · 4 / 4

State practical limitations

Ground equipment, service charges and suitable installation are required. Obstacles and weather can affect some satellite links. Evaluate these conditions against the remote site’s needs instead of promising that satellite access always works everywhere.

Work together

Choose access for a remote weather lesson

A field station needs to send observations and join occasional video calls. There is no suitable local cable or cellular service, but a satellite service covers the site.

Work together · 1 / 3

Identify the useful property

Satellite coverage can provide a connection where the available terrestrial alternatives do not.

Work together · 2 / 3

Follow the path

Ground equipment sends an uplink; the satellite system relays the communication; a downlink reaches the next ground part of the network.

Work together · 3 / 3

Evaluate the activity

Video calls are sensitive to interaction delay, so the school must consider latency as well as data capacity, equipment and cost.

A remote-access decision can favour reach while acknowledging delay and installation needs.

Check

Try it yourself · Teacher-written

Describe the satellite relay path and explain why a high data rate does not guarantee a short conversation delay.

Check your reasoning

Data travels from ground equipment on an uplink, is relayed through the satellite system and travels down to ground equipment on a downlink. Data rate measures bits per second; signal travel time and other delays are separate, so a high-capacity link can still have noticeable latency.

Connect

Keep this idea

Satellite links extend reach through radio relay paths with their own distance, equipment and service constraints.

Communicate data · Concept 09

Justify the carrier from movement, distance and environment

Observe

Four links with different jobs

JobKey requirement
Desktop to classroom equipmentShort fixed connection
Tablet around the roomMovement within coverage
School buildingsRequired capacity, route and interference conditions
Remote field stationReach where terrestrial access is unsuitable

Look, then discuss

Could the same medium be the best answer for every job without knowing the conditions?

Discuss the observation

A recommendation starts with requirements. A property matters when it changes the success, cost or reliability of this particular connection.

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 4

Extract the constraints

Identify which endpoints move, the route length, available infrastructure, obstacles, interference, required capacity, acceptable delay, installation cost and maintenance. Use facts supplied by the scenario rather than inventing a universal ranking.

Explanation · 2 / 4

Link a mechanism to a consequence

Fibre carries light and avoids electromagnetic interference in its medium. WiFi uses radio and permits movement within coverage. A microwave link can avoid trenching but needs a suitable directional path. Satellite can serve a remote area while introducing different equipment and delay constraints.

Explanation · 3 / 4

Compare alternatives using the same factor

If mobility is the requirement, compare a trailing wired connection with usable wireless coverage. If interference is the problem, compare the signal carriers under that environment. “Fibre is fast; copper is cheap” is incomplete unless those factors are linked to the actual requirement.

Explanation · 4 / 4

Retain a limitation

A justified answer can prefer an option while stating its installation or service limitation. A mixed school network can use different media for different links; there is no requirement for the whole system to use one carrier.

Work together

Choose two complementary school connections

Students move with tablets in a classroom. The classroom equipment connects to a distant building along an electrically noisy route.

Work together · 1 / 3

Choose the moving-client link

WiFi supports tablets moving within suitable radio coverage. Check interference, capacity and access controls.

Work together · 2 / 3

Choose the building link

Fibre is suitable for the noisy route because it carries light rather than an interference-sensitive electrical signal; choose equipment for the stated distance and required capacity.

Work together · 3 / 3

Explain why the choices differ

The first link prioritises client mobility. The second prioritises a fixed route and resistance to electromagnetic interference.

A complete design can combine carriers, each justified by its own job.

Question 1 · Command word: Compare6 teacher marks

Compare a wired connection with WiFi for tablets used around a warehouse in terms of mobility, transmission conditions and physical installation.

S2.07.A01S2.08.A03
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. A wired tablet is constrained by its cable length and connection point.
  2. WiFi allows the tablet to move within the access point coverage area.
  3. A cable provides a guided signal path and avoids sharing the local radio spectrum.
  4. WiFi can suffer interference, obstacles and competition for radio capacity in the warehouse.
  5. Wired access requires a cable run and network port at each working position.
  6. WiFi reduces end-device cabling, although access points still need installation and connection to the LAN.

Common error: Use the same comparison factors for both options; a wired connection is not immune to all faults.

Question 2 · Command word: Explain4 teacher marks

Explain suitable transmission choices for (a) a 500 m link across an electrically noisy factory, (b) moving classroom tablets and (c) fixed buildings with a clear aligned path where cables cannot be installed. Give one limitation of the choice for the buildings.

S2.07.A01S2.08.A02S2.08.A03S2.08.A04S2.08.A06
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. (a) Fibre is suitable for the distance and is not susceptible to electromagnetic interference in its light carrier.
  2. (b) WiFi gives moving tablets cable-free access within coverage.
  3. (c) A directional microwave link can connect the fixed buildings without installing cable across the gap.
  4. The microwave link needs a suitable unobstructed aligned path, so a new obstruction can impair it.

Common error: State the deciding property of each scenario, not a universal media speed ranking.

Question 3 · Command word: Describe10 teacher marks

Describe the characteristics of copper cable, fibre-optic cable, radio waves including WiFi, microwaves and satellites.

S2.08.A01S2.08.A02S2.08.A03S2.08.A04S2.08.A05
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Copper carries electrical signals through a guided cable path.
  2. Copper is relatively inexpensive but suffers attenuation and electromagnetic interference.
  3. Fibre carries pulses of light through glass or plastic.
  4. Fibre provides high bandwidth, low attenuation and immunity to electromagnetic interference, but installation and termination can cost more.
  5. WiFi uses radio waves through space and provides mobility within its coverage area.
  6. WiFi is affected by distance, obstacles, interference, shared spectrum and wireless-security requirements.
  7. Microwaves use an aligned directional beam between fixed antennas and can provide a high-bandwidth point-to-point link without cable between the sites.
  8. Microwaves normally need line of sight and can be weakened by obstruction, misalignment, interference or bad weather.
  9. Satellites use an uplink and downlink to provide very wide coverage, including remote or moving locations.
  10. Satellite communication can be affected by time delay, bad weather and higher equipment or service cost.

Common error: Do not describe every wireless medium as WiFi; distinguish local radio coverage, directional microwaves and a satellite uplink/downlink.

Question 4 · Command word: Suggest6 teacher marks

Suggest a medium for a high-capacity link between two school buildings and a different medium for laptops that move around classrooms. Justify each choice and give one drawback of each.

S2.07.A01S2.08.A02S2.08.A03S2.08.A06
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Fibre-optic cable is suitable between the buildings.
  2. Its high capacity and low attenuation support the inter-building link.
  3. Fibre installation and termination can be expensive.
  4. WiFi is suitable for the classroom laptops.
  5. Radio access allows the laptops to move within coverage without individual trailing cables.
  6. Obstacles, interference or shared radio capacity can reduce the wireless connection quality.

Common error: One medium need not solve every part of the scenario.
