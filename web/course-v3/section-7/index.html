Section 7 · Ethics and ownership

Build it. Use it. Take responsibility.

A school wants a reading assistant that turns printed words into speech. Follow the people who design it, the permissions needed to use its software, and the effects of relying on AI.

Start with the people →

One project, connected questions

01

Build a reading assistant people can trust

A school wants a camera-based reading assistant that can recognise printed words, translate them and read them aloud. Begin with the people who depend on it and the responsibilities of the professionals who build it.

02

Make and justify a responsible decision

The reading assistant raises a practical question: should the school release a version with a known limitation? Identify the people affected, compare realistic actions and justify a response that changes when the evidence changes.

03

Choose software with the rights the school needs

The school wants to adapt the reading assistant and share its version with partner schools. Follow the separate questions of ownership, permission, source access, price, trial conditions and support before choosing a licence.

04

Follow the AI, then evaluate its effects

Open the reading assistant’s information flow: a camera image becomes recognised text, translated words and speech. Then examine access, costs and environmental effects before deciding how the school should use it.

Learn in manageable sections

Each suggested session allows 45 minutes for explanation, discussion and practice. Take extra time when needed; a topic can span several sessions. Classroom shows one step at a time. Full reading keeps the complete explanations together.

  1. Session 1 · 45 minutes suggested

    Why a reading assistant creates responsibilities

    Observe the school case, explain the need for ethics and turn competence, honesty and confidentiality into actions.

    • Why does technical knowledge create responsibility? →
    • Turn professional duties into practical actions →
  2. Session 2 · 45 minutes suggested

    Professional standards and support

    Distinguish a code’s purpose from membership benefits, learn BCS and IEEE, and attempt the two related past-paper parts.

    • Use professional standards and support →
  3. Session 3 · 45 minutes suggested

    People and the consequences of choices

    Identify duties to colleagues and the public, then compare reporting and concealment using the decision activity.

    • Identify who is affected and what they need →
    • Trace the consequences of two actions →
  4. Session 4 · 45 minutes suggested

    Justify and reconsider an ethical action

    Match controls to risks, reconsider the location example when its purpose changes, and apply duties in a past-paper question.

    • Choose an action and test it against changed conditions →
  5. Session 5 · 45 minutes suggested

    Copyright and software freedoms

    Separate ownership from permission and explain the FSF freedoms through adaptation and sharing.

    • Separate owning software rights from using a copy →
    • Understand what “free” means in free software →
  6. Session 6 · 45 minutes suggested

    Source, trials and business terms

    Distinguish open-source permissions from visible source, then separate shareware, commercial activity, proprietary restrictions and support; apply the distinctions to the selected past-paper parts.

    • Check permissions as well as visible source →
    • Separate a trial, a price and a business model →
  7. Session 7 · 45 minutes suggested

    Choose from the school’s requirements

    Compare complete offers, change the requirements in the activity and apply licence and copyright reasoning to the past paper.

    • Match the complete plan to the offered terms →
  8. Session 8 · 45 minutes suggested

    What makes a task an AI task?

    Compare storage with recognition and distinguish learning, checking and inference using the pump and page examples.

    • Recognise the intelligent task →
  9. Session 9 · 45 minutes suggested

    From a photographed page to speech

    Predict each intermediate representation, operate the pipeline and diagnose errors at the stage supported by evidence.

    • Follow a page from pixels to spoken words →
  10. Session 10 · 45 minutes suggested

    Explain applications through their information flow

    Transfer input, inference, output and use to maintenance, healthcare, fraud detection, recommendations and sorting.

    • Transfer the input-to-use method →
  11. Session 11 · 45 minutes suggested

    Access, fairness and privacy

    Explain distinct accessibility benefits, inspect group performance and attempt the reading-device past-paper question after learning its social effects.

    • Who gains access, and who still faces a barrier? →
  12. Session 12 · 45 minutes suggested

    Productivity, money and work

    Calculate recurring and initial costs, change the review workload and distinguish valued time from cash and employment claims.

    • Compare useful work with the complete cost →
  13. Session 13 · 45 minutes suggested

    Measure the environmental comparison

    Include the added system in operating totals, change conditions and identify the evidence needed for a broader lifetime judgement.

    • Compare resource savings with the system’s footprint →
  14. Session 14 · 45 minutes suggested

    Make the final school recommendation

    Combine mechanisms, responsibilities, permissions and impact evidence into a supported deployment decision; reconsider it when safeguards change.

    • Decide how the school should use the assistant →
