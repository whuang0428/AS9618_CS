Knowledge explanation

Explain, model and apply

01

The topology is the link pattern

S2.04.A01S2.04.A02S2.04.A03S2.04.A04S2.05.A01

A bus connects devices to one shared backbone. A signal travels along the common medium and attached devices inspect it.

A star gives each end device a separate link to one central switch. A frame travels from the source to the switch and then from the switch to the destination link.

A mesh provides multiple interconnections. A full mesh connects every node pair directly; partial meshes retain several, but not all, possible links. Alternative connected routes may remain after one link fails.

A hybrid combines two or more topology patterns. Always name the combined patterns and trace the actual segments used.

Count links first; trace the path second

Bus
  1. Sender places the signal on the shared backbone.
  2. The signal propagates along the common cable.
  3. The addressed host accepts it.
Star
  1. Source sends the frame on its own link to the switch.
  2. The switch reads the destination address.
  3. The switch forwards on the destination’s separate link.
Full mesh
  1. Source router reads the destination.
  2. Routing information selects a direct or indirect route.
  3. A different connected route may be selected after a link failure.
Hybrid
  1. Source sends to its local switch.
  2. The frame crosses the inter-switch link.
  3. The remote switch forwards to the destination.
Technical reading rule

The reviewed image establishes the link pattern. The numbered path is authoritative: count each physical segment and name every forwarding device.

Common misconception

Peer-to-peer is a role model, not a mesh topology.

A star does not contain one device-to-device cable running through the switch.

02

A good choice connects structure to consequence

S2.05.A02S2.05.A03

A star usually isolates one end-device cable failure, but the central switch is a single point of failure for that star.

A bus uses less cable but shares the backbone and traffic; a backbone break can divide or stop the network.

A mesh can offer alternative routes, but extra links and ports increase cost, installation and management.

A hybrid can match different areas, but its failure behaviour depends on the constituent topologies and the links joining them.

Justification checklist

Path

Which links and devices does the packet use?

Failure

Exactly what becomes unreachable?

Traffic

Is the medium shared or switched?

Cost

How many links, ports and devices are required?

Growth

How is another device or segment added?

Common misconception

‘Mesh is best’ is not a justification; resilience must be weighed against link and management cost.

教师讲法 / 易错点

每个 topology 让学生完成同一句型：Because the links are…, if…, therefore…。

Application5 marks

Trace a frame from a computer on the left star segment to a computer on the right star segment in the illustrated hybrid. Then state the effect of the inter-switch link failing.

S2.04.A04S2.05.A01S2.05.A02
Answer and marking points
  1. Source computer to local switch.
  2. Local switch to inter-switch link.
  3. Inter-switch link to remote switch.
  4. Remote switch to destination computer.
  5. The two segments cannot communicate across the failed link, although local communication within each functioning star may continue.

Common error: Do not claim every computer powers off; describe communication reachability.

Exam-style6 marks

Recommend a topology for a classroom where cable faults should normally affect only one workstation and new workstations must be easy to add. Include one drawback.

S2.04.A02S2.05.A02S2.05.A03
Answer and marking points
  1. Recommend star.
  2. Each workstation has a separate cable.
  3. One end-device cable failure normally isolates only that workstation.
  4. A new device can be connected to an available switch port.
  5. Frames are forwarded through the central switch.
  6. Central switch failure affects the star or extra switch/cabling costs are stated.

Common error: A named topology without consequences does not justify the choice.

This is an original, structurally equivalent task. It does not reproduce Cambridge question or mark-scheme wording.

Original equivalent task

A hospital needs a topology between four critical routing nodes. Draw or describe a design with an alternative route after one link fails, then justify the design and trace one packet route.

Build the answer

  1. Choose a mesh and state which nodes are connected.
  2. Give one valid source-to-destination route.
  3. Give a different connected route after a named link failure.
  4. Link resilience to the hospital requirement.
  5. Acknowledge extra links, ports, cost or management.

How marks are earned

  • A route must follow links that exist in the stated topology.
  • A justification earns marks when the structural feature produces a relevant consequence.

Common losses

  • Drawing duplicate or missing mesh links.
  • Saying ‘more reliable’ without showing the alternative path.
