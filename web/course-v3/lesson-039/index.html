Make and justify a responsible decision

The reading assistant raises a practical question: should the school release a version with a known limitation? Identify the people affected, compare realistic actions and justify a response that changes when the evidence changes.

Look, then discuss

Who is affected when a colleague finds a fault in the reading assistant?

A colleague discovers that the tool misreads small print. The project leader proposes removing the failed result and the colleague’s name from the report. List the separate effects on the colleague, students and the school before deciding what should happen.

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 4

Name the decision before naming people

A stakeholder is a person or group affected by a decision or with an interest in it. Here the decision is whether to disclose or conceal a known limitation and whether to credit the person who found it. Starting with the actual decision prevents a vague list of people with no explained connection.

Explanation · 2 / 4

Include responsibilities towards colleagues

Fair treatment, recognising contributions, constructive criticism and helping colleagues develop are professional responsibilities. Acknowledge who found the fault. Discuss the test evidence and suggest specific improvements to the work. A personal attack or removing deserved credit can discourage people from raising future concerns.

Explanation · 3 / 4

Include users and the wider public

Students need realistic claims and reliable information about the tool’s limits. Their safety, privacy and fair treatment matter even when they did not purchase the product. A parent can also be a stakeholder because a decision affects their child, although the parent never operates the assistant.

Explanation · 4 / 4

Include the client or employer without ignoring other duties

The school needs competent advice to decide how to spend money, support students and manage the release. Reporting a limitation lets it plan a correction or alternative. A deadline matters to the school, but it does not remove responsibilities towards colleagues or the public.

Work together

Write a reason for two different groups

The prompt asks for responsibilities towards colleagues and the public. Use the discovered small-print fault.

Work together · 1 / 2

Develop the colleague point

The programmer should acknowledge the colleague’s contribution and offer constructive criticism of the proposed repair so that the colleague can improve the work. This addresses the team relationship and the quality of the development.

Work together · 2 / 2

Develop the public point

The programmer should be honest about the small-print limitation so that students and teachers do not trust the assistant for a reading it has not reliably produced. This addresses the people using or depending on the output.

An explained responsibility identifies an action, its recipient and why the action matters.

Check

Explain it yourself · Teacher-written

The colleague never uses the finished assistant. Does that remove them from the stakeholder analysis? Give one responsibility towards them.

Check your reasoning

No. Decisions about credit, criticism and treatment affect the colleague’s professional work. Acknowledge their contribution or give specific constructive feedback so that their work can improve. Using the product is not a requirement for being affected.

Next, keep the fault fixed and compare what happens under two different choices.

Check a common misunderstanding

Writing “be ethical to society” leaves both the action and its effect unexplained.

Ethics and ownership · Concept 02

Trace the consequences of two actions

Observe

Look, then discuss

Can an ethical action cost money immediately and still be the justified choice?

Move to an independent example: a route labelled wheelchair-accessible includes steps. The developer knows this before launch. Compare disclosing and correcting the label with concealing the fault and publishing it unchanged. Keep the original fault the same in both branches.

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 4

Make the causal link explicit

A consequence explains how an action changes what happens to someone. Reporting the steps allows the route label to be corrected or withdrawn; a wheelchair user can then avoid an unsuitable route. The phrase “good reputation” skips this mechanism. Explain why honest information provides a better basis for trust.

Explanation · 2 / 4

Recognise the cost of the ethical action

Investigating and correcting the route may delay launch and require staff time. An ethical response can therefore impose an immediate cost. That cost belongs in the comparison, together with the benefit of avoiding a foreseeable misleading claim.

Explanation · 3 / 4

Explain the consequences of concealment

Concealment may allow the operator to meet the launch date. A traveller may then reach steps, need assistance or be unable to complete the journey. Complaints and loss of confidence may follow if users learn that the fault was known. These are plausible consequences supported by the situation, not guaranteed outcomes for every journey.

Explanation · 4 / 4

Compare the same conditions fairly

Do not make the error disappear in one branch unless the chosen action corrects it. Do not assume a delay automatically removes all future risk. Compare actions using the evidence supplied and distinguish immediate effects from later possibilities.

Work together

Return to the reading assistant

The school can delay a small-print feature and supply a checked transcript, or advertise the current feature as fully reliable despite known failures.

Work together · 1 / 3

Explain the responsible branch

Disclosing the limitation and keeping a checked transcript takes preparation time. Students retain a usable source, and teachers can avoid relying on the uncertain output.

Work together · 2 / 3

Explain the concealment branch

Claiming complete reliability avoids the immediate correction work but can cause students to learn or act on incorrect text. The school may later have to correct materials and rebuild confidence in its claims.

Work together · 3 / 3

Support the comparison

The available checked transcript makes disclosure practical. The deadline advantage does not justify withholding information that affects students’ use of the material.

Compare benefits and harms for each action; an immediate organisational advantage does not settle whether that action is responsible.

Check

Explain it yourself · Teacher-written

A learner writes “Reporting the fault is ethical because the company always earns more money.” What needs correcting?

Check your reasoning

Ethical reporting may cost money or delay release. Its justification should connect accurate disclosure to affected people and responsible decisions. Long-term financial benefit is possible, but it is not guaranteed and is not the only reason to report a fault.

A judgement must now choose a workable action and explain the conditions that make it appropriate.

Check a common misunderstanding

A benefit in one branch does not cause a harm in the other. They are outcomes of alternative actions.

Ethics and ownership · Concept 03

Choose an action and test it against changed conditions

Observe

Look, then discuss

Does a useful purpose justify collecting every piece of information that might be available?

The school wants to keep every photograph taken by the reading assistant indefinitely “in case it helps later”. Some photographs contain student names and personal notes. The immediate purpose is to read the current page aloud. Compare retaining all images with processing the current page and deleting it when no longer needed.

Explain

Explanation · 1 / 4

State the purpose and the evidence

Begin with the actual aim: making the current text accessible. Establish what information the function requires and what is known about its use. “It may be useful one day” is too vague to explain why identifiable images must be retained indefinitely.

Explanation · 2 / 4

Compare a workable alternative

A less intrusive option must still meet the stated purpose. If the assistant can read a page without retaining a permanent identifiable copy, that offers the immediate benefit with less stored personal information. If a separate improvement study needs examples, assess that purpose and its conditions explicitly instead of treating all future use as already justified.

Explanation · 3 / 4

Match safeguards to risks

Restricting access reduces who can view stored images. A retention limit reduces how much old information remains available. Removing unnecessary identifiers can reduce exposure, but does not automatically make every image anonymous. Checking and correcting an inaccurate output addresses a different risk. Explain the mechanism of each safeguard.

Explanation · 4 / 4

Give a conditional, supported conclusion

Proportionality means matching the action to the need. State who should do what, why the action fits the evidence and what uncertainty remains. A reasonable conclusion can be to proceed under specific controls, run a limited trial, obtain further evidence or reject the proposed use.

Work together

Change the purpose: attendance or an emergency?

A college proposes tracking students’ phones all day and night to confirm arrival at lessons. Later, one student becomes separated from a supervised outdoor trip.

Work together · 1 / 3

Decide for ordinary attendance

A classroom check-in can meet the attendance purpose without retaining an off-site movement history. Use that narrower method, restrict access and provide a correction route for failed check-ins.

Work together · 2 / 3

Reconsider for the missing student

A classroom check-in cannot locate the missing student. A suitable available location service may now meet an urgent safety purpose within the trip’s authorised arrangements. Limit access to the responsible search team and check whether the location is current.

Work together · 3 / 3

Limit the changed decision

End the emergency collection when it is no longer needed and handle remaining records according to the justified follow-up purpose. The emergency does not establish a reason for permanent tracking of everyone.

The justified action can change when the purpose and evidence change. The duty to explain necessity, effects and limits remains.

Check

Explain it yourself · Teacher-written

A proposal adds “human review” to the assistant’s output. What must you establish before treating this as an effective safeguard?

Check your reasoning

Establish who reviews it, whether they can compare it with a reliable original, whether they have the skills and time to identify errors, and whether they can correct the result before reliance. Merely naming a reviewer does not explain how the risk is reduced.

Original question · Cambridge 9618/12

1. Apply ethical responsibilities to two groups

Cambridge 9618/12 · October/November 2024 · 5(a) · 4 marks

Selected parts: 5(a) [4].

Hint — reading the question

Address colleagues and the public separately. A long answer about only one group is capped at two marks.

Reasoning and model answer — teacher-written

Towards colleagues, the programmer should give constructive criticism so that their colleagues can improve their work. Towards the public, the programmer should be honest and realistic about what the software can do so that the public can trust the claims made about it.

Official mark scheme
Why this earns marks — teacher explanation

The scheme allows at most two marks for colleagues and two for the public. Each sentence gives an action and its consequence. Other accepted actions include fair treatment, giving credit and protecting public health or personal information.

Common mistakes — teacher analysis

A general statement such as “be ethical to everyone” does not show how the responsibility applies to either group.

Question 1 · Command word: Identify2 teacher marks

A town publishes named residents' complaints on an open website to show how quickly repairs are handled. Identify two stakeholders and explain one concern for each.

S7.03.A01
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Residents who complained may have private contact or complaint details exposed.
  2. Town staff need to report performance without losing residents' trust through unnecessary disclosure.

Common error: Explain how each group is affected rather than listing unrelated organisations.

Question 2 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

A developer knows that a delivery app sends drivers to a closed bridge. Explain one consequence for drivers of reporting the error and one of concealing it.

S7.03.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Reporting the defect allows the route to be corrected or warned against, helping drivers avoid wasted or unsafe journeys.
  2. Concealing it leaves drivers relying on a route that cannot be completed, causing delay and possible safety risks.

Common error: Use both actions and make the consequence specific to drivers.

Question 3 · Command word: Explain3 teacher marks

A response to an attendance proposal says, 'Tracking everyone is good because it is efficient.' Explain two weaknesses in this reasoning and suggest an alternative to continuous location tracking.

S7.03.A01S7.03.A03
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. The claim does not identify whose efficiency improves or how an attendance action causes that improvement.
  2. Continuous location collection affects students’ privacy even outside the attendance decision, and its necessity has not been established.
  3. Record arrival at the classroom instead: this still establishes attendance while collecting less information about students’ movements.

Common error: Critique the supplied reasoning rather than inventing a different response.

Question 4 · Command word: Explain3 teacher marks

A programmer’s colleague finds an error in the programmer’s code for a public timetable. The programmer plans to dismiss the criticism and claim sole credit for the repair. Explain two responsible actions towards the colleague and one towards the public, giving a reason for each.

S7.03.A01S7.03.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Discuss and accept evidence-based criticism constructively so that the error and the technical work can be improved.
  2. Acknowledge the colleague’s contribution to finding or repairing the error so that credit is fair and the colleague’s work is recognised.
  3. Correct the timetable or disclose its known limitation so members of the public do not unknowingly rely on incorrect journey information.

Common error: Address both recipients. Praise without a relevant action does not repair the professional problem.

Question 5 · Command word: Justify4 teacher marks

A library wants to publish borrowers’ names and loan histories to recover overdue books. Private reminders reach those borrowers. Later, a named borrower privately authorises a librarian to share the title of one lost book with a replacement supplier. Justify a response to each proposal and state one safeguard and one remaining risk for the supplier disclosure.

S7.03.A01S7.03.A03
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Use private reminders for overdue loans: they meet the stated purpose without exposing every borrower’s reading history.
  2. The later limited disclosure has a different stated purpose and the borrower’s authorisation; share only what the supplier needs for that replacement rather than treating it as permission to publish all loans.
  3. Verify the intended supplier/recipient and limit the information to the required book details, excluding unrelated borrowing history.
  4. A recipient could still retain or misuse the details; communicate the limited purpose and review what information genuinely needs to be sent. The control reduces rather than eliminates risk.

Common error: Neither “never share anything” nor “consent allows every future use” evaluates both sets of conditions.

Question 6 · Command word: Explain2 teacher marks

A company postpones a booking-system launch to correct a fault that loses reservations. Explain one short-term cost and one longer-term benefit of this decision.

S7.03.A02
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Postponement may delay booking revenue or require extra development expenditure.
  2. Fixing the fault reduces lost reservations and helps retain customers' confidence in future bookings.

Common error: Ethical behaviour does not imply an absence of immediate costs.

Question 7 · Command word: Justify3 teacher marks

A sports club needs emergency contacts for one weekend trip but proposes keeping them indefinitely for advertising. Justify a more appropriate use of the data and suggest two safeguards.

S7.03.A03
Answer and checking points — teacher-written
  1. Limit use to the trip's emergency purpose because advertising is a separate use the stated need does not justify.
  2. Restrict access to staff responsible for emergencies.
  3. Delete the list after the trip and any justified follow-up period instead of retaining it indefinitely.

Common error: Each safeguard must address access, purpose or retention in this scenario.
