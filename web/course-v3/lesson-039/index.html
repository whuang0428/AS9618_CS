Ethical decisions and their consequences

Identify stakeholders, compare the consequences of two courses of action and justify a response to a specific ethical problem.

How can an ethical judgement show exactly who is affected and why?

Before you start

A response says only 'The programmer is unethical.' What evidence is missing?

Check your answer

It must identify the action, the relevant duty and an effect on a named stakeholder.

Teaching checkpoints
  • Answer each knowledge-unit check before revealing its explanation.
  • Use the worked scenario to connect a claim to evidence, then attempt the practice and independent exam questions.

Core explanation

Identify affected people and interests

Begin with the actual decision: what is someone proposing to collect, disclose, change or conceal? Identify direct stakeholders, such as users and employees, and others affected indirectly, such as families or the wider public. Different groups may have different interests in the same system.

Compare interests using the circumstances

Connect each stakeholder to a relevant responsibility. A school handling location data has duties towards pupils' privacy as well as safety. Naming 'society' without explaining a connection is too vague to support a judgement. Benefits and harms are parallel possibilities to investigate; one is not automatically the cause of the other.

Common misconception

A possible benefit does not automatically cause the possible harm; analyse each effect separately.

Check your understanding

Why is a parent a stakeholder even if they never operate the tracking system?

Check your answer

The system's decisions can affect their child's safety and privacy, so the parent has an interest in its use.

02

Compare ethical and unethical actions

S7.03.A02

Visual overview

Core explanation

Follow the effect of each action

Explain consequences through a cause-and-effect link: the action changes what happens to a stakeholder. For example, disclosing a known defect allows a client to avoid relying on unsafe output; concealing it can lead the client to make a harmful decision with misplaced confidence.

Compare options and their conditions

Consider both courses of action. Acting ethically can have an immediate cost, such as delay or extra testing, while preserving safety and trust. Acting unethically may produce a short-term advantage but can cause harm, complaints, loss of confidence or professional consequences. State consequences supported by the situation rather than assuming that every outcome is certain.

Worked example · A travel application's accessibility error

  1. Situation

    A developer finds that a route labelled wheelchair-accessible includes steps. The manager wants to publish the route unchanged to meet a launch date.

  2. Ethical course

    Correct or withdraw the label and tell the travel operator about the uncertainty. Launch may be delayed, but wheelchair users can avoid an unsuitable route.

  3. Unethical course

    Keep the label and hide the report. A user may reach an inaccessible location and need assistance; the operator may receive complaints and lose trust.

  4. Comparison

    The deadline benefit does not remove the duty to provide reliable accessibility information. The correction addresses a foreseeable consequence for a specific group.

Common misconception

Simply writing 'good reputation' or 'bad reputation' leaves out the action and why it changes trust.

Check your understanding

Can an ethical response still cost the organisation money?

Check your answer

Yes. Correcting a problem may cost money or delay a release while preventing harm and supporting justified trust.

03

Justify a proportionate response

S7.03.A03

Visual overview

Build a supported judgement

  1. DecisionIdentify the proposed action and legitimate purpose.
  2. EvidenceConnect benefits, harms and duties to affected people.
  3. AlternativeTest whether a less intrusive option meets the purpose.
  4. ConclusionChoose an action with relevant conditions and remaining limits.

Core explanation

Use evidence and relevant duties

Use evidence to weigh the stated purpose against the effects on people. Compare a realistic alternative that could meet the purpose with less intrusion or risk. Proportionality means the action should be appropriate to the need and no more intrusive or harmful than necessary to achieve it.

Support the recommendation

A conclusion should identify an action and explain why it is justified. Relevant safeguards might include limiting collection and retention, restricting access, correcting inaccurate information or providing a way to challenge a decision. A safeguard needs a mechanism linked to the particular risk, and it may reduce rather than eliminate that risk.

Worked example · Continuous tracking for attendance

  1. Proposal

    A college wants students' phones to report location all day and night, but its stated aim is to confirm arrival at lessons.

  2. Alternative

    A check-in at the classroom can record attendance without keeping an off-site movement history.

  3. Judgement

    Use classroom check-in with restricted access and a short retention period. Continuous overnight tracking is unnecessary for this attendance purpose.

  4. Remaining issue

    Check-in errors still need a correction process so a failed scan does not unfairly mark a student absent.

Common misconception

A balanced response need not treat all choices as equally acceptable or end with an unsupported compromise.

Check your understanding

How does a short retention period address a privacy concern?

Check your answer

Deleting records after their stated purpose ends reduces the amount of historical information available for misuse.

Cambridge command words

Practice questions

Question 1 · Command word: Identify2 marks

A town publishes named residents' complaints on an open website to show how quickly repairs are handled. Identify two stakeholders and explain one concern for each.

S7.03.A01
Answer and marking points
  1. Residents who complained may have private contact or complaint details exposed.
  2. Town staff need to report performance without losing residents' trust through unnecessary disclosure.

Common error: Explain how each group is affected rather than listing unrelated organisations.

Question 2 · Command word: Explain2 marks

A developer knows that a delivery app sends drivers to a closed bridge. Explain one consequence for drivers of reporting the error and one of concealing it.

S7.03.A02
Answer and marking points
  1. Reporting the defect allows the route to be corrected or warned against, helping drivers avoid wasted or unsafe journeys.
  2. Concealing it leaves drivers relying on a route that cannot be completed, causing delay and possible safety risks.

Common error: Use both actions and make the consequence specific to drivers.

Question 3 · Command word: Explain2 marks

A company postpones a booking-system launch to correct a fault that loses reservations. Explain one short-term cost and one longer-term benefit of this decision.

S7.03.A02
Answer and marking points
  1. Postponement may delay booking revenue or require extra development expenditure.
  2. Fixing the fault reduces lost reservations and helps retain customers' confidence in future bookings.

Common error: Ethical behaviour does not imply an absence of immediate costs.

Question 4 · Command word: Justify3 marks

A sports club needs emergency contacts for one weekend trip but proposes keeping them indefinitely for advertising. Justify a more appropriate use of the data and suggest two safeguards.

S7.03.A03
Answer and marking points
  1. Limit use to the trip's emergency purpose because advertising is a separate use the stated need does not justify.
  2. Restrict access to staff responsible for emergencies.
  3. Delete the list after the trip and any justified follow-up period instead of retaining it indefinitely.

Common error: Each safeguard must address access, purpose or retention in this scenario.

Question 5 · Command word: Explain3 marks

A response to an attendance proposal says, 'Tracking everyone is good because it is efficient.' Explain two weaknesses in this reasoning and suggest an alternative to continuous location tracking.

S7.03.A01S7.03.A03
Answer and marking points
  1. It does not identify whose efficiency improves or how the claimed improvement occurs.
  2. It ignores students' privacy and whether continuous monitoring is necessary for attendance.
  3. Recording arrival at the classroom can meet the attendance need with less collection of movement data.

Common error: Critique the supplied reasoning rather than inventing a different response.

Question 1 · Command word: Identify · 4 marks

A school plans to display named pupils' medical conditions on a public dashboard to help trip organisers. Identify two affected groups and explain one professional responsibility towards each.

Answer and marking points
  • Pupils whose conditions are listed are affected because public disclosure can expose sensitive personal details.
  • The school should keep those details available only to people who need them for the trip.
  • Trip organisers are affected because they rely on medical information to respond safely.
  • The school should supply organisers with accurate, relevant information through an appropriate restricted channel.

Common error: Connect each responsibility to a named group and the proposed disclosure.

Question 2 · Command word: Explain · 4 marks

An accountant reports that a payroll program underpays overtime. Its developer can report the fault immediately or hide it until after a bonus is paid for meeting the release date. Explain a consequence for employees and a consequence for the employer for each course of action.

Answer and marking points
  • Immediate reporting allows employees' missing overtime payments to be identified and corrected.
  • The employer must spend time or money correcting payroll promptly but can reduce continuing payment disputes.
  • Concealment can leave employees receiving less pay than they earned.
  • The employer may face growing complaints and loss of staff trust when the known error is discovered.

Common error: Cover both stakeholders under both actions; do not award four marks for repeating that money is lost.

Question 3 · Command word: Justify · 3 marks

A library wants to prevent overdue loans by publishing borrowers' names and reading histories. Private reminders would also reach overdue borrowers. Justify a course of action and explain one safeguard and one limitation of your proposal.

Answer and marking points
  • Use private reminders because they address late returns without exposing reading histories to the public.
  • Keep reminder records accessible only to authorised library staff, reducing inappropriate disclosure.
  • A wrong address may still send a reminder to the wrong person, so contact details and delivery errors need review.

Common error: A valid alternative conclusion needs evidence that addresses the privacy harm and the stated purpose.
