Security, privacy, integrity and the need for protection

Core explanation

Data security protects information against unauthorised access, loss or damage. Privacy concerns the appropriate collection, use and disclosure of personal information. Data integrity concerns accuracy, completeness and consistency, including avoiding incorrect changes during processing or transfer.

These concepts can be affected separately. Restricting access does not make an incorrect value true, and copying a record perfectly does not make its disclosure appropriate. Identify the property affected from what happened to the data, not simply from the presence of a password.

Common misconception

One incident may affect more than one property; the terms are not interchangeable.

Check your answer

Integrity: restricting access cannot correct a date that was entered wrongly.

Core explanation

A system must remain available to authorised users and must execute trusted operations. Malware, stolen credentials or damaged hardware can disrupt processing or expose data even when a separate file copy has been protected.

Data also needs protection wherever copies are stored or transferred. A secured computer cannot prevent disclosure from an unencrypted removable drive that has already been lost. Combine system controls with protection of the data and recoverable copies.

Worked example · A clinic cannot open its records

  1. Situation

    The record files are encrypted, but malware prevents the appointment computer from starting.

  2. Consequence

    Staff cannot use the records even though encryption still protects the files from casual reading.

  3. Response

    Restore the system from a trusted source and recover records from tested backups; encryption alone cannot restore the service.

Common misconception

Confidentiality alone is not enough when the service is unusable.

Check your understanding

Does locking the computer room protect a data copy sent to the wrong recipient?

Check your answer

No. Physical protection of the computer does not control a copy after disclosure.

Cambridge command words

Practice questions

Question 1 · Command word: Identify3 marks

Identify the principal data concern in each case and justify it: a school publishes private medical details; a marks total is calculated incorrectly; an intruder destroys stored files.

S6.01.A01
Answer and marking points
  1. Privacy: medical information is disclosed inappropriately.
  2. Integrity: the recorded result is inaccurate.
  3. Security: an unauthorised person causes data loss.

Common error: Justify each classification from the event; accept a further property only with a valid explanation.

Question 2 · Command word: Explain2 marks

Explain why password protection does not ensure that an entered examination mark is accurate. Give an example of a protected but inaccurate value.

S6.01.A01
Answer and marking points
  1. A password restricts access but does not compare the entered mark with its source.
  2. For example, an authorised teacher may enter 64 instead of the actual 46.

Common error: A password is not a validation or verification check.

Question 3 · Command word: Explain2 marks

Explain why a company must protect its computer system as well as encrypt its stored customer files. Give two different consequences of system compromise.

S6.02.A01
Answer and marking points
  1. Malware may prevent authorised staff from running the customer application.
  2. An attacker controlling an authenticated session may read or alter data after the application decrypts it.

Common error: Relate the consequence to the running system rather than repeating that data is valuable.

Question 4 · Command word: Explain2 marks

Explain why securing a server room is insufficient when staff carry unencrypted copies of records on removable drives.

S6.02.A01
Answer and marking points
  1. The removable copy is outside the protection of the server room.
  2. Someone finding the drive can read its contents unless the copy itself is protected.

Common error: Consider the location of each copy.

Question 1 · Command word: Explain · 2 marks

A survey database accurately stores named respondents' answers. An employee sends the unchanged records to an advertiser without permission. Explain the privacy and integrity implications separately.

Answer and marking points
  • The disclosure uses personal information for an unauthorised recipient or purpose, so privacy is affected.
  • The unchanged answers may retain their accuracy and completeness; a privacy breach does not itself show corruption.

Common error: Do not infer that accurate information may be disclosed without restriction.

Question 2 · Command word: Explain · 3 marks

A museum's encrypted catalogue survives an incident, but its only workstation cannot start. Explain why encryption has not preserved a usable service and suggest a suitable recovery measure.

Answer and marking points
  • Encryption protects readable contents rather than restoring a working operating system or application.
  • Staff still require a functioning computer to retrieve and process the catalogue.
  • A tested system backup or replacement workstation can restore the processing environment.

Common error: The recovery measure must address the failed system.

Question 3 · Command word: Explain · 3 marks

A shop allows only managers to edit stock records. One manager enters 90 instead of 9. Explain what the access control achieved, what it did not achieve, and one check that could detect this error.

Answer and marking points
  • The edit was restricted to an authorised role.
  • The permission check did not establish the correctness of the quantity.
  • Comparing the saved quantity with the original stock count can expose the mismatch.

Common error: An authorised change can still be incorrect.

Lesson review

Summary

Security

Protect against unauthorised access, loss and damage.

Privacy

Control appropriate use and disclosure of personal information.

Integrity

Preserve accurate, complete and consistent records.

Two protection targets

Keep the processing service usable and protect its data copies.
