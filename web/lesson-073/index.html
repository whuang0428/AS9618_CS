Lesson 073 | 45 minutes | Paper 1 Section 7 | Ethics and ownership

Data can be useful. It can also become a diary someone never agreed to write.

This lesson focuses on privacy, surveillance and data protection. Students learn how to analyse what data is collected, why it is collected, who can access it, how long it is kept and what safeguards reduce ethical risk.

Explainprivacy, surveillance, personal data and sensitive data
Assessrisks caused by monitoring, tracking and over-collection
Recommenddata protection safeguards with scenario-specific reasons
What data? Why collect it? Who can access it? How long kept? What could go wrong? What safeguards?

If the answer to "why do you need this data?" is "because we can", the red flag has arrived early.

Why this matters

Exam answers need consequences: privacy loss, misuse, discrimination, reduced trust, or improved safety with safeguards.

Common trap

Do not write "data protection means encrypt everything" only. Protection also includes consent, limits, access control, retention and accountability.

Warm-up hook

A fitness app asks for step count, heart rate, location, contacts and microphone access. Which request is most suspicious for its stated purpose?

The app counting your steps probably does not need to hear your lunch plans.

Knowledge point 1

Privacy is control over personal information

PrivacyThe ability to control how personal information is collected, used and shared.
Personal dataData that can identify a person directly or indirectly.
Sensitive dataData that could cause greater harm if misused, such as health, biometric or location data.
ConsentA clear agreement to a specific use of data, based on understandable information.
Chinese support: privacy = 隐私；personal data = 个人数据；sensitive data = 敏感数据；consent = 同意/授权。

Surveillance can protect people, but it changes behaviour and trust

Knowledge point 3

Data protection reduces misuse and unnecessary exposure

Data minimisationCollect only what is necessary for the stated purpose.
Purpose limitationUse data only for the purpose users were told about.
Access controlLimit who can view, edit or export personal data.
Retention limitDelete data when it is no longer needed.
TransparencyExplain what data is collected, why and how it is used.
SecurityProtect data using measures such as encryption, authentication and audit trails.
AccuracyKeep personal data correct and allow errors to be corrected.
AccountabilityRecord and justify processing decisions so misuse can be investigated.

Knowledge point 4

Good answers link a principle to a concrete risk

Principle
Plain English
Scenario use
Fair and lawful use
Do not mislead users or use data in unexpected harmful ways.
A health app should not secretly sell location patterns.
Data minimisation
Collect less when less is enough.
A step counter may not need contacts or microphone access.
Retention
Do not keep personal data forever by default.
CCTV footage should be deleted after a defined period unless needed.
Access and security
Only authorised people should access protected data.
School monitoring logs should be restricted to safeguarding staff.

Knowledge point 5

Use a privacy impact answer structure

1. DataName the personal or sensitive data being collected.
2. PurposeExplain why the organisation claims it is needed.
3. RiskDescribe misuse, over-collection, surveillance or unfair effect.
4. SafeguardRecommend a specific protection and link it to the risk.
Answer frame: The system collects [data] for [purpose]. This may affect privacy because [risk]. A suitable safeguard is [control], which reduces the risk by [mechanism].

Summary

Privacy and data protection in six exam-ready ideas

PrivacyPeople should have control over personal information.
SurveillanceMonitoring can protect people but may reduce privacy and trust.
MinimisationCollect only what is necessary for a clear purpose.
TransparencyTell users what is collected, why and how it is used.
RetentionDo not keep personal data longer than needed.
SafeguardsUse access control, encryption, audit trails and accountability.
