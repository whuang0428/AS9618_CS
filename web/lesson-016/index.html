Why this matters

Network questions often look descriptive, but marks come from precise distinctions and scenario-linked consequences.

Common trap

LAN does not mean “small and wireless”. LAN means a local network, usually privately owned within a limited area.

Warm-up hook

The classroom printer is shared by 24 computers. What is the actual network purpose?

Pick the purpose first. The printer is not being dramatic; it is simply a shared resource.

Knowledge point 1

Why networks are used

Communication

Users and devices exchange data, messages, files and requests.

Resource sharing

Printers, storage, internet connections and software can be shared instead of duplicated.

Central management

Accounts, permissions, backups and updates can be controlled from central systems.

Collaboration

Multiple users can work with shared files or services, sometimes at the same time.

Chinese support: resource sharing = 资源共享；central management = 集中管理；collaboration = 协作。

How a shared resource becomes useful

  1. Mechanism: A device packages a request and names the destination.
  2. Reason: The network carries that request to the shared resource.
  3. Result: One managed resource can then serve many authorised devices.

Analogy: A library serves many readers because requests reach one organised collection.

Boundary: If the path or shared service fails, many users lose access together.

Knowledge point 2

LAN vs WAN

Feature
LAN
WAN
Area
Limited area, such as a room, building or school site.
Large geographical area, such as cities, countries or continents.
Ownership
Usually owned and managed by one organisation or individual.
Often uses infrastructure from telecommunications providers.
Example
A school network connecting classrooms and servers.
A bank connecting branches in different countries.

Why LAN and WAN management differs

  1. Mechanism: A LAN usually stays within one organisation's controlled site.
  2. Reason: A WAN crosses distance and often uses provider-owned infrastructure.
  3. Result: More owners and routes add latency, cost and fault-finding complexity.

Analogy: Managing one campus is different from coordinating transport across several cities.

Boundary: Wi-Fi does not make a network a WAN; scale and control do.

Knowledge point 3

Network topologies

Bus

Devices share one backbone cable. Less cabling reduces cost, but traffic and failures are concentrated on the shared path.

Star

Each device has an independent link to a central switch. Cable faults are isolated, but switch failure affects every attached device.

Full mesh example

Every pair of devices has a direct link, creating alternative paths. Reliability increases, but links and ports grow rapidly.

Hybrid

Two or more topology types are combined. The design can balance local simplicity, expansion and wider resilience.

Why connection patterns change risk

  1. Mechanism: The layout determines which physical paths data can follow.
  2. Reason: Shared paths reduce cabling but concentrate traffic and failures.
  3. Result: Alternative paths improve resilience but require more links and ports.

Analogy: Road layouts trade construction cost against alternative routes after a closure.

Boundary: No topology is universally best; cost, scale and failure tolerance decide.

Mistake debugging

Fix the weak network answer

“A WAN is just a bigger LAN.”

A WAN covers a large geographical area and often uses telecommunications provider infrastructure; a LAN is local and usually privately managed.

“Star topology is always best because it is modern.”

Star topology is useful because devices connect through a central switch, making faults easier to isolate; however, the central switch is a potential single point of failure.

Extra practice

Packet movement through network topologies

In a bus, a transmitted signal travels along the shared backbone and devices inspect it. In a star, each frame travels through the central switch. In a mesh, alternative links can provide several possible routes. A hybrid combines behaviours of its component topologies.

Topology justification must connect packet path to the scenario: central failure, cable failure, congestion, expansion and redundancy are consequences of the structure.

Worked example: One star cable fails

Only the device on that cable loses its link; packets between other devices still pass through the central switch. If the switch fails, all attached paths fail.
