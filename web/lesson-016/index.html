Common trap

LAN does not mean “small and wireless”. LAN means a local network, usually privately owned within a limited area.

Warm-up hook

Knowledge point 1

Why networks are used

Communication

Users and devices exchange data, messages, files and requests.

Resource sharing

Printers, storage, internet connections and software can be shared instead of duplicated.

Central management

Accounts, permissions, backups and updates can be controlled from central systems.

Collaboration

Multiple users can work with shared files or services, sometimes at the same time.

Knowledge point 3

Network topologies

Bus

Devices share one backbone cable. Cheap, but the backbone can become a bottleneck or single point of failure.

Star

Devices connect to a central switch or hub. Easy to add devices, but central device failure affects the network.

Mesh

Devices have multiple paths between them. Reliable and fault tolerant, but expensive and complex to install.

Ring

Devices are connected in a loop. Data travels around the ring; a break may disrupt communication unless bypassed.

Mistake debugging

Fix the weak network answer

“A WAN is just a bigger LAN.”

A WAN covers a large geographical area and often uses telecommunications provider infrastructure; a LAN is local and usually privately managed.

“Star topology is always best because it is modern.”

Star topology is useful because devices connect through a central switch, making faults easier to isolate; however, the central switch is a potential single point of failure.

Extra practice

Packet movement through network topologies

In a bus, a transmitted signal travels along the shared backbone and devices inspect it. In a star, each frame travels through the central switch. In a mesh, alternative links can provide several possible routes. A hybrid combines behaviours of its component topologies.

Topology justification must connect packet path to the scenario: central failure, cable failure, congestion, expansion and redundancy are consequences of the structure.

Worked example: One star cable fails

Only the device on that cable loses its link; packets between other devices still pass through the central switch. If the switch fails, all attached paths fail.
