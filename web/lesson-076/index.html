Lesson 076 | 45 minutes | Paper 1 Section 7 | Ethics and ownership

Computing changes the world. Sometimes the electricity bill is only the first clue.

Computing affects energy use, e-waste, work, access to services, communication, inclusion and inequality. Good exam answers balance benefits with harms and connect each point to a specific stakeholder or consequence.

Technology Stakeholders Benefit Harm Mitigation Judgement

"Technology improves life" is not an answer. It is a poster waiting for evidence.

Why this matters

Environmental and social impact questions reward balanced evaluation, not a list of dramatic opinions.

Common trap

Do not claim computing is simply good or bad for the environment. The impact depends on use, energy source, lifespan and disposal.

Warm-up hook

A company moves meetings online and buys new laptops for every worker. Is this environmentally positive?

The answer has more branches than a badly planned flowchart.

Choose one. Look for more than one impact.

Knowledge point 1

Environmental impacts include energy use and resource consumption

Energy useDevices, networks and data centres consume electricity during operation.
Carbon emissionsEmissions depend on electricity source, device manufacture and transport.
Resource extractionManufacturing hardware requires metals, rare materials, water and energy.
LifespanShort upgrade cycles increase manufacturing and disposal impact.
Chinese support: environmental impact = 环境影响；energy use = 能源使用；carbon emissions = 碳排放；resource extraction = 资源开采。

Knowledge point 2

E-waste is an ethical issue, not just a rubbish-bin issue

E-wasteDiscarded electronic devices and components.
Toxic materialsSome components contain harmful substances that can pollute soil or water if mishandled.
RecyclingResponsible recycling can recover materials and reduce landfill, but must be managed safely.
Reuse and repairExtending device life reduces demand for new manufacturing.

Knowledge point 4

The digital divide is about access, skills and inclusion

Device accessSome users cannot afford suitable hardware.
ConnectivityReliable high-speed internet is not equally available.
Digital skillsUsers may lack confidence or training to use online systems.
AccessibilitySystems should support users with disabilities or different needs.

Knowledge point 5

Use a balanced impact structure

1. ChangeName the computing change, such as automation or online services.
2. BenefitExplain a positive impact for a named stakeholder.
3. HarmExplain a negative impact with a realistic consequence.
4. MitigationSuggest a practical way to reduce harm.
Answer frame: [Technology] benefits [stakeholder] because [reason]. However, it may harm [stakeholder] by [consequence]. This can be reduced by [mitigation].

Extra practice

AI applications and impact chains

AI applications include medical image classification, recommendation, fraud detection, language processing, autonomous control and predictive maintenance. A valid impact answer names the AI decision mechanism and traces a consequence for a stakeholder.

Social impacts include access, bias and privacy; economic impacts include productivity, job redesign and error cost; environmental impacts include data-centre energy/material use and optimisation of transport or power. Evaluation balances benefits, harms and mitigations in context.

Worked example: Medical triage model

The model prioritises scans, reducing waiting time, but biased training data may miss a patient group. Human review, representative validation data and monitoring can reduce rather than eliminate the risk.

Targeted practice

1. Give one economic benefit of predictive maintenance.

Show answer

Reduced downtime or repair cost by predicting failure.

2. Give one environmental cost of training a large model.

Show answer

Electricity use and associated emissions/resource demand.

3. Why can biased training data create social harm?

Show answer

The model may produce systematically poorer decisions for under-represented groups.

Exam-style question

4 marks

Evaluate the use of AI to select applicants for jobs.

Show MS
  1. B1 valid benefit such as speed/consistent initial processing
  2. B1 valid risk such as bias, opacity, privacy or exclusion
  3. B1 explains a stakeholder consequence
  4. B1 balanced judgement or mitigation linked to the context

Summary

Environmental and social impacts in six exam-ready ideas

EnergyDevices, networks and data centres use electricity.
E-wasteShort device lifespans create disposal and toxic-material risks.
WorkAutomation and remote work can help some workers and harm others.
AccessOnline services can improve access but widen the digital divide.
MitigationUse recycling, repair, efficient design, training and inclusive access.
BalanceGive both benefit and harm before reaching a judgement.

Homework

Practise balanced impact evaluation

Task 1: Impact table Choose one technology: online learning, automation, cloud storage or remote work. Make a table with environmental benefit, environmental harm, social benefit, social harm and mitigation.
Task 2: Rewrite weak claims Rewrite: "Online services are better because they are convenient." Add stakeholder, exclusion risk, environmental consideration and one mitigation.
Task 3: Timed response Answer in 8 minutes: "Evaluate the environmental and social impacts of replacing face-to-face services with online services." Include a balanced conclusion.
