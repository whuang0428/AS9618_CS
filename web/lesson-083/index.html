Lesson 083 | 45 minutes | Paper 1 Section 8 | Database design

Normalisation is database tidying with consequences. It stops one changed email from becoming a treasure hunt.

This lesson explains why database designers normalise data: to reduce unnecessary duplication, avoid update, insertion and deletion anomalies, and keep related data in appropriate tables. We focus on design reasoning, not SQL queries.

Common trap

Knowledge point 1

Purpose of normalisation

Normalisation is the process of organising data into well-structured tables to reduce unnecessary redundancy and improve data integrity.

Reduce redundancyA fact such as TutorEmail is stored once in Tutor, not repeated in every course row.
Improve integrityFewer repeated facts means fewer chances for inconsistent versions.
Prevent anomaliesInsertion, update and deletion problems become less likely.
Clarify dependenciesFields should depend on the key of their own table, not on unrelated fields.
Chinese support: normalisation = 规范化；redundancy = 冗余；anomaly = 异常；dependency = 依赖关系。

Knowledge point 3

Three anomaly types

Update anomalyA repeated fact is updated in some rows but not others, so inconsistent data remains.
Insertion anomalyA new fact cannot be stored until another unrelated fact exists, such as a new course with no enrolled student.
Deletion anomalyDeleting a row accidentally removes the only stored copy of a separate fact.

Knowledge point 4

Normal form idea, without turning the lesson into a maze

AS-level questions usually expect the purpose and effect of normalisation more than a formal proof. Still, the normal form idea helps explain the method.

1NFValues are atomic; each field stores one value, not a repeating list.
2NF ideaNon-key fields should depend on the whole key, not part of a composite key.
3NF ideaNon-key fields should not depend on other non-key fields.
Keep this practical: identify the fact, decide which entity it describes, then move it to that table.

Extra practice

First, second and third normal form

1NF requires atomic values and no repeating groups. 2NF is 1NF with every non-key attribute dependent on the whole primary key, removing partial dependencies. 3NF is 2NF with no non-key attribute dependent on another non-key attribute, removing transitive dependencies.

Normalisation decomposes tables while preserving keys and relationships. A 3NF design stores each fact once in the table identified by its determinant, reducing insertion, update and deletion anomalies.

Worked example: Order line data

ORDER_LINE(OrderID, ProductID, ProductName, Quantity) has composite key OrderID+ProductID. ProductName depends only on ProductID, so split PRODUCT(ProductID, ProductName) and ORDER_LINE(OrderID, ProductID, Quantity) to reach 2NF for that dependency.

Targeted practice

1. What does 1NF remove?

Show answer

Repeating groups and non-atomic/multiple values in one field.

2. What dependency violates 2NF?

Show answer

A non-key attribute depending on only part of a composite key.

3. What dependency violates 3NF?

Show answer

A non-key attribute depending on another non-key attribute.
